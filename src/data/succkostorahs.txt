# The Days Between Yom Kippur and Sukkot — and the Festival of Sukkot
## A Comprehensive Summary of a Multi-Author Chassidic Anthology

---

## Overview

This text is an anthology of teachings from dozens of Chassidic masters — spanning from the Shelah HaKadosh (R' Yeshaya Horowitz, early 17th century) through the Lubavitcher Rebbe (R' Menachem Mendel Schneerson, 20th century) — organized around two interlocking themes: **(1) the spiritual significance of the four days between Yom Kippur and Sukkot**, and **(2) the nature of Sukkot itself** as a festival of joy, atonement's culmination, and cosmic repair. The central argument running through all the teachings is that these periods are not incidental transitions on the calendar, but are among the most charged moments of the entire year — a time of heightened holiness, spiritual opportunity, deep teshuvah, and ultimately transformative joy.

---

## Part One: The Days Between Yom Kippur and Sukkot (י"א–י"ד תשרי)

### Section 1 — These Days Are Like a Festival

**Shelah HaKadosh (R' Yeshaya Horowitz)** opens the discussion by establishing that the four days between Yom Kippur and Sukkot are themselves quasi-holy days, akin to חול המועד. The reason: the Jewish people are immediately engaged in מצות סכה and the ארבעה מינים, and this sacred engagement brings a person to זכירה — to remembering his דבקות in Hashem and cleaving to Him. The Midrash Tanchuma (cited in the Tur, Hilchot Rosh Hashanah §581) already noted that after Yom Kippur one is busy with the mitzvot of sukkah and lulav and does not sin — therefore the first day of Sukkot is called **"ראשון לחשבון עוונות"** — the first day for the accounting of sins (i.e., sins begin to be counted only from that point).

He adds: These days stand between two sanctities — the holiness of Yom Kippur and the holiness of Sukkot — and therefore one must conduct oneself with an additional degree of elevation, just as Rabbeinu Yonah (Sha'arei Teshuvah, Sha'ar 2, §14) wrote that the seven days between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur have the status of חול המועד.

---

**Amrei Pinchas (R' Pinchas of Koritz):** He said of himself that for three days after Yom Kippur he could not hear what people said to him — as though they were speaking to a piece of wood. The Zohar Hakadosh (Vol. 1, 221a) supports this: after Yom Kippur, the illumination of that day continues to shine for three days, and in that radiance Hakadosh Baruch Hu forgives Israel's sins even of those three days. Because people are occupied with the sukkah and lulav on the eve of Yom Tov, the first day of Sukkot is therefore called "ראשון לחשבון עוונות."

---

**Derashot Chatam Sofer (R' Moshe Sofer):** Yom Kippur is considered a **"יומא אריכתא"** — an extended day — stretching until Sukkot. Since the atonement of Yom Kippur continues through the four intervening days, and further atonement is achieved through the ארבעה מינים and leaving one's home for the sukkah (an act of exile that atones), Yom Kippur is reckoned as if it extends until the first day of the festival.

---

**Avodat Yisrael (R' Yisrael of Kozhnitz):** The four days correspond to the letter ד of the Divine name **א-ד-נ-י**, with the entire period from Tisha B'Av to Rosh Hashanah corresponding to נ, the ten days of teshuvah to י, the four days between Yom Kippur and Sukkot to ד, and the first day of Sukkot — called "ראשון" in the verse (Leviticus 23:40) — corresponding to the א.

---

**Ginzei Yosef (R' Yosef of Stanov):** The four days correspond to the four letters of the name הויה, and represent Hakadosh Baruch Hu's blending of מדת הרחמים with מדת הדין to sustain the world. As the Midrash (Kohelet Rabbah 9:1) states: at the conclusion of Yom Kippur a heavenly voice goes forth: "לך אכול בשמחה לחמך" — *"Go, eat your bread with joy."* Therefore anyone whose worries whether his deeds are acceptable before Hashem can now feel confident. Just as a person who knows his business ventures are successful can eat with an expansive mind, so Israel — having received the explicit Torah promise (Leviticus 16:30) **"כי ביום הזה יכפר עליכם"** — *"For on this day atonement shall be made for you"* — can now eat with joy.

---

### Section 2 — The Joy of These Days

**Torat Emet (R' Yehuda Leib Eiger of Lublin):** He elaborates on the great joy that should prevail in these days, particularly during the Shabbat meal that falls within them. The verse (Kohelet 9:7) — **"לך אכול בשמחה לחמך כי כבר רצה האלהים את מעשיך"** — *"Go, eat your bread with joy, for G-d has already approved your deeds"* — is said specifically about these days.

He offers a profound distinction: for Pesach, Shavuot, and Sukkot, reasons for the holiness are given in the Torah tied to historical events (the clouds of glory, Matan Torah), and one might think the power is limited to those original events. But for Yom Kippur, the Torah writes in the **future tense**: **"יכפר... תטהרו"** — *"he will atone... you will be purified"* — indicating that this power never changes; it is an eternal promise. And from this we can boldly begin to renew our deeds without the bitterness of sins — because the joy after Yom Kippur is proportional to the depth of the pain one felt before it.

He adds a beautiful gematria: the ראשי תיבות of כי כבר רצה האלהים את מעשיך together with the letters = the numerical value of **רפואה** — healing. For healing is the rectification of something that went wrong — returning it to its former state. And that is precisely what teshuvah accomplishes.

He further cites Deuteronomy 28:47 — **"תחת אשר לא עבדת את ה' אלקיך בשמחה"** — *"Because you did not serve Hashem your G-d with joy"* — indicating that joyful service is obligatory. And Hashem has given us days of joy at the beginning of the year for precisely this reason (Sukkot 5643).

---

**Sefat Emet (R' Yehuda Aryeh Leib of Ger):** After Yom Kippur one must accept the service of Hashem with joy, just as in the Wilderness when Hakadosh Baruch Hu forgave the sin of the Golden Calf and the Mishkan was established — the people drew near to the Creator's service with joy and enthusiasm. So too on Sukkot. In the Temple era, when the scarlet thread turned white, Israel was filled with joy; now too we must believe that we have been purified. And the sign of whether this is true is how much joy one feels internally (Sukkot 5600).

---

### Section 3 — These Days as Four Days of Inspection (בקור)

**Be'er Moshe (R' Moshe Yechiel of Ozhorov):** Drawing on Zohar (Vol. 2, 40b) which equates the tenth of Nisan with the tenth of Tishrei (Yom Kippur), he develops a remarkable parallel:

Just as the Korban Pesach was taken on the tenth of Nisan for **four days of בקור** — inspection for blemishes — before being offered on the fourteenth, so too, immediately after Yom Kippur, four days of spiritual בקור begin. The connection is established by the Zohar's linking of these two passages, and is reinforced by the gezeirah shavah of ט"ו–ט"ו between Pesach and Sukkot (Sukkah 27b).

Moreover, Yom Kippur itself is connected to מסירות נפש, since one diminishes one's חלבו ודמו (fat and blood) — and this is counted as a sacrifice, as the Gemara (Berachot 17a) states. Therefore, just as the Pesach lamb was taken on the tenth with great self-sacrifice (the Egyptians worshipped the lamb, creating genuine danger — hence the institution of Shabbat HaGadol), so Yom Kippur involves a parallel self-sacrifice.

After Yom Kippur, a person ascends to such a spiritual height that he can now notice sins he had previously trampled underfoot and not attended to. But this accounting requires four days — hence the four days of inspection, and the first day of Sukkot is appropriately called **"ראשון לחשבון עוונות."** On Sukkot one praises Hashem with the ארבעה מינים and is newly created — **"ועם נברא יהלל י-ה"** — *"a people created shall praise G-d"* (Psalm 102, Shocher Tov).

---

### Section 4 — The Days Prepare to Receive the Light of Yom Kippur

**Divrei Chaim (R' Chaim of Sanz):** After Yom Kippur passes our sins, we leave Egypt and journey to Sukkot — the encompassing clouds of glory — a great chesed covering us. But we cannot immediately absorb this chesed; we need preparation. The four days are branches from the root-holiness of Yom Kippur, and they receive the illumination of Yom Kippur in the secret of **אמא עילאה** — the supernal Imma — which is the aspect of **תשובה עילאה** (as the Zohar writes: "תיאובתא סגי"). Through these days one can arouse the aspect of שופר that elevates all oppressed souls back to their supernal root — as the verse (Isaiah 27:13) states: **"והיה ביום ההוא יתקע בשופר גדול ובאו האובדים... והנדחים"** — *"On that day a great shofar will be sounded and those who were lost shall come..."*

He offers a remarkable numerical observation: the word **"יום"** times four, combined with **"יום כפור"**, equals the numerical value of **שופר**.

Furthermore, the connection of Yom Kippur's holiness with Sukkot during these four days: **"יום כפור"** + four times **"סוכה"** (since one is occupied with sukkah in these days) = **שס"ד** — which is the numerical value of **השטן** (364). This draws the holiness of Yom Kippur over the 364 days of the year on which the שטן cannot rule. (The word **תשובה-אהבה** = the numerical value of **"יום כפור"** + twice **"סוכה"**.) This teshuvah of love transforms willful sins (זדונות) into merits.

---

**Ahavat Yisrael (R' Yisrael of Vizhnitz):** The four days between Yom Kippur and Sukkot are especially propitious for **תיקון פגם היסוד**. He notes that four times "סוכה לולב" equals the numerical value of **"תיקון יסוד"**.

---

**Menachem Tzion (R' Menachem Mendel of Rimanov):** The צדיקים of the generation, during these days, begin to do teshuvah afresh, and thereby attain the level called **"יד חזקה"** — the strong hand. This is alluded to in Isaiah 46:12: **"שמעו אלי אביריי לב רחוקים מצדקה"** — which the Gemara (Berachot 14b) reads as referring to the צדיקים who pierce all barriers with strength and force, not for themselves but for all of Israel. If one adds the four days of Sukkot to the ten days of teshuvah, one gets **יד** (= 14) — **יד החזקה**. And therefore, immediately afterward during the festival, we take the ארבעה מינים — our weapons against all accusers. In Temple times, seventy bulls were offered to suppress the seventy nations; now we read their parasha and offer our lips instead.

---

**Likkutei Sichot (R' Menachem Mendel of Lubavitch):** He cites the famous words of his predecessor, the Rebbe Rashab (R' Shalom Dovber), who said the morning after Yom Kippur: **"איצטער ערשט דארפן תשובה טאן"** — *"Now is the time to do teshuvah."* This is the Rebbe Rashab's chiddush — that the essence of teshuvah begins precisely after Yom Kippur.

---

### Section 5 — The Value of Preparation for the Mitzvah

**Shem MiShmuel (R' Shmuel of Sochatchov):** He elaborates on a profound distinction that he heard from the Alexander Rebbe: **When one is occupied with the actual performance of a mitzvah, the divine light is clothed in a vessel (כלי)**. But when one is occupied in the *preparation* for a mitzvah, **the light is unclothed** — and what is without clothing is greater, since clothing conceals the light. Furthermore, the טרדה — the busy-ness of preparation — encompasses the entire body of a person, whereas the actual performance of the mitzvah affects only the particular limb or capacity directly involved. This explains why these four days, when people are busy *preparing* sukkah and lulav, are spiritually greater than Yom Tov itself when the mitzvot are actually performed.

---

**Torat Emet (R' Yehuda Leib Eiger of Lublin):** He proves from the verse "ולקחתם לכם ביום הראשון" that preparation for a mitzvah has independent halachic and spiritual value — superior even to the mitzvah's actual performance. During Yom Kippur, Hakadosh Baruch Hu reconciles with Israel and extends the forgiveness forward (based on Psalm 85:9: **"אשמעה מה ידבר האל ה' כי ידבר שלום אל עמו ואל חסידיו"**). And in these four days, Hakadosh Baruch Hu does not count sins against Israel because they are occupied in preparations for the mitzvot of sukkah and lulav — even though the actual time for their performance has not yet arrived. **The engagement and preparation for a mitzvah is enormously precious to Hashem.**

He invokes a kal vachomer from Yoma 29a: if sinful thoughts are more severe than the sin itself, then certainly the thoughts and longing to fulfill Hashem's mitzvah are more beneficial than the mitzvah itself — and they protect the person so that he does not sin.

The logical basis: actual performance of a mitzvah requires complete purity of intention — "who can say I have purified my heart?" — but at the moment of preparation, every Jewish soul is certainly willing and ready to perform the mitzvah in complete perfection when its time arrives.

## Part Two: The Festival of Sukkot — Chag HaSukkot

### Section 6 — The Essential Nature of the Festival

**Yosher Divrei Emet (R' Meshulam Feivush of Zbarizh):** On Sukkot, the pleasure and joy that come from the purification of the soul through Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur are revealed. He cites the Ari's teaching (Sha'ar HaKavanot, Sukkot, Drush 3) that the period from Rosh Hashanah through Yom Kippur corresponds to **"שמאלו תחת לראשי"** — the left arm beneath the head — and from Yom Kippur to Sukkot corresponds to **"וימינו תחבקני"** — *the right arm embracing.* After the judgments have been sweetened, the teshuvah was performed in concealment because of the broken heart — but now the pleasure and joy emerge. This is **חג הסכות — זמן שמחתנו**.

He offers a beautiful mashal: when a father and mother wish to discipline their child and raise the strap over him, he falls beneath their wings, weeping and seeking forgiveness, clinging to them. They hold the strap until they see he is crying truly and bitterly — then they throw down the strap and take him under their wings. This is the meaning of "שמאל דוחה וימין מקרבת" (Sotah 47a). The strap was the judgments of Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur; the sukkah is the embrace — Hakadosh Baruch Hu bringing us under His wings.

The ארבעה מינים represent the awakening and spreading of חסדים after the sweetening of the דינים. All the Torah and mitzvot have one purpose — the awakening of the divine light, sometimes through חסדים and sometimes through גבורות. Hashem alone knows how to awaken the supernal illumination at each particular time; **"הוא הידוע והוא הדעת"** — He is the Knower and He is the Knowledge.

---

**Sefat Emet (R' Yehuda Aryeh Leib of Ger):** The primary request during Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur was to merit receiving the presence of the שכינה during Sukkot. As he wrote on the verse (Genesis 33:17): *"And Esav returned to Seir, and Yaakov traveled to Sukkot"* — the entire benefit of the days of teshuvah is to enable Israel to receive the face of the שכינה during Sukkot. This is the meaning of Psalm 27:4 — **"אחת שאלתי מאת ה'"** — one thing I asked — which is **"שבתי בבית ה'"** — to dwell in the house of Hashem during the seven days of the festival, to receive the face of the שכינה.

---

**Likkutei Torah (R' Shneur Zalman of Liadi — the Alter Rebbe):** The purpose of the concealment and judgment of Rosh Hashanah is so that the person should seek the divine revelation — and then find it in great joy on Sukkot. The verse (Psalm 81:4) — **"תקעו בחדש שופר בכסה ליום חגנו"** — *"Blow the shofar at the new moon, in concealment, on our festival day"* — means: Hashem conceals Himself on Rosh Hashanah in order that the joy of revelation on Sukkot be all the greater. For joy is proportional to the transition from concealment to revelation.

He develops this through the Priestly Blessing (Numbers 6:24-26): **"יאר ה' פניו אליך"** — *Hashem illuminate His face toward you* — is the forgiveness and turning away of the face of anger; **"ישא ה' פניו אליך"** — *Hashem lift His face toward you* — is the lifting away of the face of wrath so that the divine revelation can emerge. The entire arc from Rosh Hashanah through Sukkot traces this movement from concealment to revelation.

---

**Degel Machaneh Ephraim (R' Moshe Chaim Ephraim of Sudylkov):** He offers a masterful mashal: a king set up many barriers before his palace — walls, fire, and rivers — all illusions — to test which of his children would push through to reach him. The foolish child feared the barriers. The wise child understood that a loving father could not truly hide from his children, that these were illusions, and pressed through. The moment he stepped into the "river" (with self-sacrifice), the illusion dissolved and he passed through. And when one pushes through all the barriers and comes to the King — one ascends to a far higher level than before.

He connects this to **"ואנכי הסתר אסתיר פני ביום ההוא"** (Deuteronomy 31:18): when one understands that "Anochi" — the divine Essence — is present even within the concealment, then **"אסתיר"** = **"אסתר"** = Hadassah (whose numerical value with the kollel = חיים), and one emerges to bask in **"אור פני מלך חיים"**. The secret of Rosh Hashanah is **"בכסה"** — concealment; the secret of Sukkot is **"בסכה"** — the revealed divine light within the lulav and esrog.

---

### Section 7 — The Joy of Sukkot (זמן שמחתנו)

**Sfas Emes (R' Yehuda Aryeh Leib of Ger):** Sukkot is an experience like sitting in Gan Eden. The original purpose of creation was for man to dwell in the Garden — **"וישם שם... האדם"** (Genesis 2:8) — where the great joy existed (as we say: **"כשמחך יצירך בגן עדן"**). Although man was expelled (Genesis 3:24), there are times when the illumination of Gan Eden shines forth. Hashem brings us into this dwelling upon which His name rests.

---

**Kol Yaakov (R' Yaakov Tzvi Yelles):** On Sukkot one can draw down good bounty through the teshuvah of the preceding ten days — as he heard from the Chozeh of Lublin: **"לית דעתיר מחזירא"** (Shabbat 155b) — there is none richer than one who returns in teshuvah. The verse "ולקחתם לכם ביום הראשון" — you can take and draw down all good things — because you have been doing teshuvah until now.

---

**Kedushat Levi (R' Levi Yitzchak of Berditchev):** This is one of the most celebrated interpretations in the entire collection. He establishes:

- During the ten days of teshuvah and Yom Kippur, the teshuvah is **מיראה** — out of fear. The Gemara (Yoma 86b) teaches: teshuvah from fear transforms זדונות (willful sins) into שגגות (unwitting sins).
- On Sukkot, when Israel engages joyfully in the mitzvot of sukkah, lulav, and ארבעה מינים, giving charity generously with love and gladness — this constitutes **תשובה מאהבה** — teshuvah from love.
- And teshuvah from love transforms willful sins **into merits (זכיות)**.

Therefore: Hakadosh Baruch Hu, in His great compassion, actually **counts and tallies the sins** during Sukkot — specifically in order to transform them into merits! This is the meaning of **"ראשון לחשבון עוונות"**: the *חשבון* (accounting) is not an indictment but a treasure-counting — Hashem opening the books of sins to convert them all into advocates (מליצים טובים) for Israel. Before Sukkot, the sins were merely שגגות and there was nothing to count; on Sukkot, when they become זכיות, they are worth counting.

---

### Section 8 — The Mashal of the Battle (Ginzei Yosef)

**Ginzei Yosef (R' Yosef of Stanov)** offers an elaborate mashal spanning all the Yamim Noraim:

A king about to go to war summoned his soldiers and promised reward to all who fight bravely. He specifically offered **not** to give all the reward at once — lest victory lead to complacency and a subsequent battle be lost (כמשה ישורון ויבעט). Instead he promised partial reward during the festival, with the full reward to follow.

The soldiers divided into four camps:
1. Those who fight purely from love of the king, not for reward
2. Those who fight from love of the king and also love of reward
3. Those who fight purely for worldly reward
4. Those who fight purely from fear of punishment

**The nimshal:** On Rosh Hashanah there is a great battle against the accusers of Israel (the seventy nations' heavenly advocates, as described in the Zohar). The four camps of Israel correspond to:
- **Hadas** (myrtle, fragrance without taste) → those who desire only the World to Come
- **Lulav** (date palm, taste without fragrance) → those who desire this world's reward
- **Esrog** (both taste and fragrance) → those desiring both worlds' reward
- **Aravah** (neither taste nor fragrance) → those who act purely from love without any reward

All four enter the sukkah — which represents Hakadosh Baruch Hu as the shield and shelter of Israel (Psalm 121:5: **"ה' צלך"**). The *letters of "לפשע"* (for transgression) — ל (esrog, heart), פ (aravah, lips), ש (lulav, spine), ע (hadas, eyes) — which were the primary sites of sin — are now rearranged into **"לשפע"** — abundance. Therefore we take and bless with the ארבעה מינים in the sukkah — so that each blessing (ברכה) creates שפע in the upper and lower worlds, transforming the letters of transgression into the letters of abundance.

The joy is distributed across all seven days through שמחת בית השואבה, but is held back from being complete (since Hoshana Rabbah is still in progress), until **שמיני עצרת** — when the joy reaches its fullness. This explains why שמיני עצרת is both a rule unto itself and תשלומין דראשון — the completion of the first day's joy — because the entire arc of Sukkot was building toward this complete wholeness.

### Section 9 — ראשון לחשבון עוונות — Multiple Interpretations

The phrase "ראשון לחשבון עוונות" generated an extraordinary wealth of interpretation:

| Teacher | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **Be'er Moshe** | Four days of spiritual inspection parallel to the four days of Korban Pesach inspection |
| **Kedushat Levi** | Hashem opens the book of sins to transform them into merits through teshuvah me'ahavah |
| **Mareh Yechezkel** | Four days = three Patriarchs' merit + David's merit (as king, he cannot waive his honor) — only on the 15th when all four are exhausted does the accounting begin |
| **Arei Pinchas** | Repeated sins damage the חבל של קדושה more severely; only after multiple repetitions does a true reckoning begin |
| **Ginas Egoz / Pardes HaMelech** | The esrog = the sin of Adam HaRishon with the אתרוג (halachic ruling = esrog); this is "ראשון" because we are counting sins from the very first sin in history |
| **Ben Binyamin** | The שטן comes to accuse from each of the four directions on consecutive nights; Hashem turns away His face. By the night of the 15th all four directions have been used and Hakadosh Baruch Hu can no longer avoid the reckoning |
| **Derech HaMelech (R' Kalonymus Kalman of Piaseczna)** | "ראשון לחשבון עוונות" refers to the person himself — who only after Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur becomes master of himself, and for the first time can genuinely conduct the חשבון |
| **Tiferet Banim** | The Rebbe of Munkatch: this is the "first day" that a person can truly calculate sin — before this, a person lacks the self-mastery to do so, as his thoughts were not his own |

### Section 10 — The Three Regalim: Pesach, Shavuot, Sukkot — Structure and Comparison

Several teachers place Sukkot in the larger context of the three pilgrimage festivals:

**HaYashar VeHaTov (R' Tzvi Hirsch of Liska):** The three festivals correspond to three boundaries: מאיין (from where), לאן (to where), and המממצע (the middle path). Pesach is the departure from the סטרא אחרא to holiness — hence יציאת מצרים. Shavuot is the path taken — the holy Torah, the middle path. And Sukkot is the destination — dwelling beneath the שכינה, **"לחזות בנועם ה' ולבקר בהיכלו"** — *to behold Hashem's pleasantness and to visit in His sanctuary.*

**Torat Emet (R' Yehuda Leib Eiger of Lublin):** Pesach represents the departure from the 49 gates of impurity — thus its essence is *guarding against* chametz (the negative aspect). Sukkot represents the entry into the 49 gates of holiness — thus the sukkah dimensions of 7×7 טפחים = 49 square טפחים correspond to the 49 gates of holiness. On Pesach the people had only אמונה and bitachon, without yet feeling the entry into holiness. On Sukkot they already feel all the dimensions of holiness — **"למען ידעו דורותיכם כי בסכות הושבתי"** — *so that your generations should know that I caused you to dwell in sukkot.*

---

### Section 11 — The Sukkah's Spiritual Structure and Meaning

**Lev David (the Chida — R' Chaim Yosef David Azulai):** He offers an extensive kabbalistic analysis. The sukkah's gematria = **יאהדונהי** — the combination (שילוב) of הויה and אדני. The sukkah encompasses the entire person from head to foot including garments — representing the total dwelling of the שכינה with the person. He connects the four species to the four limbs that are primary (heart, spine, eyes, lips), and argues that through sitting in the sukkah — which is all temporary (עראי) — one cultivates genuine humility and הכנעה, which leads to complete subservience to the Creator.

**Pele Yoetz (R' Mordechai Dov of Hornostypol):** He distinguishes between two levels of יראה:
- **יראה חיצונית** — external awe: the awe one feels before a great king, deriving from his outer magnificence
- **יראה פנימית** — internal awe: the בושת — shame — one feels before a great person's inner essence

Rosh Hashanah cultivates יראה חיצונית — awakening the fear of Hashem's sovereign rule. Yom Kippur cultivates יראת בשת — shame before one's own sins. And Sukkot with the ארבעה מינים represents the **integration** of both. The sukkah corresponds to יראת בשת — as the ראשי תיבות of בסכות תשבו שבעת ימים spell **בשת**. The ארבעה מינים correspond to the spread of Hashem's sovereignty — **"מלך שמו נקרא"**.

---

### Section 12 — Special Themes: The Sukkah as Remedy; the Waving of the Lulav

**Bnei Yissachar (R' Tzvi Elimelech of Dinov):** He develops an extended mashal comparing the entire sequence from Rosh Hashanah through שמיני עצרת to a person recovering from a severe illness:

1. **Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur** = the cutting out of the wound and removal of all the pus (the sins)
2. **The days of Sukkot** = the daily healing of the wound — each day the accounting of sins diminishes
3. **The ארבעה מינים** = the bandage applied to the incision, healing the heart (חזה) which was struck through confession
4. **שמחת בית השואבה** = the drawing of fresh air during recuperation — hence "שואבים רוח הקודש" — they would draw the holy spirit, because drawing fresh air is part of the healing
5. **Hoshana Rabbah** = the completion of healing
6. **שמיני עצרת / שמחת תורה** = the person now fully healed can eat abundantly, rejoicing in the Torah itself

This brilliantly explains why the seventy bulls decreasing each day of Sukkot: as the sins diminish daily, the power of the seventy nations diminishes proportionally — since their power derives only from our sins.

And שמיני עצרת is both **רגל בפני עצמו** (a festival unto itself) and **תשלומין דראשון** (completing the first day) — because the healing was not complete until Hoshana Rabbah, and only now is the complete joy possible. Therefore it is simultaneously its own entity and the completion of the entire arc.

---

**Or HaChochmah (R' Uri Feivel of Dubna):** The name חג הסכות suggests **"סוכה בדבר מה"** — perceiving hints of divine wisdom in every created thing. Just as Sarah our Mother was called **"יסכה"** (Megillah 14a) because she "saw" with the holy spirit, so Sukkot's essence is: to see in every worldly delight and every phenomenon a hint of divine wisdom — **"רמיזא דחכמה"** — and to gather it back to its root. This is the meaning of **"בצאתך מגרנך ומיקבך"** — gathering from the threshing floor and the winery back to the root.

He offers a beautiful ראשי תיבות: **"חג"** = **ח**סד **ג**בורה — love and awe; these are to be perceived and gathered from everything in this world, in a refined and transparent manner like glass (זכוכית) rather than the opacity of earthenware (חרס).

---

### Section 13 — The Joy of Sukkot as Cosmic Significance

**Shom MiShmuel (R' Shmuel of Sochatchov):** Joy on Yom Tov is a great and transcendent matter. The סטרא אחרא cannot generate genuine joy — it can only steal joy from holiness (as the Zohar on כי בשמחה תצאו). Therefore the שמחת יום טוב is a protection for the hidden divine light (אור גנוז) that shines on Yom Tov. And on Sukkot particularly, when Israel needs this protection (per the Zohar: ויעקב נסע סכתה), the joy is all the more necessary as a protection. He elaborates: even the "joy" of frivolity (הוללות) has no joy of its own — it can only steal from holiness.

**R' Yisrael of Tchortkov:** If, upon arrival of Sukkot after Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, one is filled with joy — this is a clear sign that one's teshuvah was genuine and one's sins have been forgiven. For the Gemara teaches (Yoma 86a): great is teshuvah, for it reaches the Throne of Glory — meaning those who truly repented can enter the presence of Hakadosh Baruch Hu. And the sign of that nearness is joy. Therefore Hakadosh Baruch Hu commanded us to surround ourselves in the sukkah with קדושה so that any joy felt must be pure spiritual joy and not physical pleasure — only then is it a reliable sign.

---

**Likkutei Sichot (R' Menachem Mendel of Lubavitch):** While all festivals are called **"מועדים לשמחה"** — times for joy — Sukkot alone is called **"זמן שמחתנו"** — our time of joy. The explanation: Sukkot immediately follows Yom Kippur, when Israel receives the חתימה — the Divine seal — for a good and sweet year in all respects, materially and spiritually. Sukkot then is the **revelation and celebration** of that sealing. This is why the joy of Sukkot is greater than all the other Yom Tovim — because it is the public rejoicing over the good verdict received on Yom Kippur.

He adds: **the joy of all the Yamim Tovim throughout the year flows from Sukkot** — it is the source of joy for the entire year.

---

### Section 14 — Hoshana Rabbah and the Final Seal

**Sefat Emet (R' Yehuda Aryeh Leib of Ger):** The custom of Hoshana Rabbah was instituted because after Yom Kippur — when Jewish souls are purified — they must cry out to Hashem the Savior to rescue them from the יצר הרע. He bases this on the Gemara (Sanhedrin 73a): "if she cried out and there was no savior — had there been a savior, he would have been obligated even at the cost of his life to save her." Israel calls out: **"ישראל נושע בה'"** — and Hakadosh Baruch Hu must save even at the cost of the "pursuer." This is why the Anshei Knesset Gedolah cried out (Nehemiah 9:4) and were able to nullify the יצר of idolatry.

# הַסֻּכָּה — A Comprehensive Summary of Chassidic Teachings

## Overview

This text is an anthology of Chassidic teachings on the mitzvah of Sukkah, drawn from dozens of masters across multiple dynasties and generations. The central themes weave together into a rich tapestry: the Sukkah as divine protection (צִלָּא דִּמְהֵימָנוּתָא — the shelter of faith), as a vehicle for teshuvah and atonement, as a space of extraordinary sanctity rooted in the Holy Names, and as a conduit for the seven Ushpizin to pour their spiritual qualities into Israel. The discourse moves from the protective function of the Sukkah (shielding us from the forces of the סִטְרָא אַחֲרָא after the Days of Awe), through its Kabbalistic structure (composed entirely of Divine Names), to its role as a garment for the soul, a mikvaeh-like purifier, a replica of the בֵּית הַמִּקְדָּשׁ, and the fulfillment of simple and complete אֱמוּנָה.

---

## Part One: The Sukkah as Protection from the External Forces

### Sitting in the Shadow of the Holy King

The opening teaching, cited in the name of the Baal Tiferet Tzvi and students of the Gaon of Ropshitz, establishes that the Sukkah "covers over us and shields and protects all of Israel." The verse from Shir HaShirim (2:3) — **בְּצִלּוֹ חִמַּדְתִּי וְיָשַׁבְתִּי** ("In His shade I delighted and I sat") — is read as an allusion to sitting in the Sukkah, in the shade of the Holy One, blessed be He. The subsequent phrase **וּפִרְיוֹ מָתוֹק לְחִכִּי** ("and His fruit is sweet to my palate") alludes to the פְּרִי עֵץ הָדָר and the four species.

One who learns or prays inside the Sukkah is said to be sitting literally within the supernal worlds, since each world is called a "day" — hence **בַּסֻּכֹּת תֵּשְׁבוּ שִׁבְעַת יָמִים** (Leviticus 23:42): "In Sukkot you shall dwell seven days," the seven days corresponding to the seven supernal worlds. Just as those worlds shelter and cover the person during these days, they also do not abandon him in times of need.

### Rabbi Noach of Kobrin: Why Tzaddikim Make Dense Schach

Rabbi Noach of Kobrin explains why tzaddikim use thick שְׁכָך even though the law requires that stars be visible through it. He notes that the שְׁכָך corresponds to בִּינָה — and the tzaddikim are deeply rooted in בִּינָה, hence they pile it thick.

### Rabbi Shmuel of Sochatchov: The Protective Hiding Within the Sukkah

After Yom Kippur, sins are atoned — but the *existence* of the transgressions has not completely vanished. From every sin a מַלְאָךְ מַשְׁחִית (destructive angel) is created, as it says (Jeremiah 2:19): **תְּיַסְרֵךְ רָעָתֵךְ** — "Your evil will chastise you." The Gemara (Sotah 3b) teaches: **כָּל הָעוֹבֵר עֲבֵרָה אַחַת בָּעוֹלָם הַזֶּה מְלַפַּפְתּוֹ** — "Whoever transgresses a sin in this world, it wraps itself around him and walks before him to the Day of Judgment."

Kapparah (atonement) means "wiping away" — the sin is removed from clinging to the person, but its very existence has not been fully erased. Hence the destructive forces still pursue after the person to implant evil thoughts, even though they stand outside. The Sukkah, however — which is entirely composed of Holy Names (the Gemara in Sukkah 9b states that the Name of Heaven rests upon the Sukkah) — is a space the external forces simply cannot enter. More than that, they cannot even gaze upon the Sukkah, because the great light shining within it blinds their eyes.

The Midrash (Shemot Rabbah 27) brings a parable about a hunter who tries to catch a bird that has landed on the image of the king — he cannot touch it for fear of damaging the royal image. So too, the person hidden within the Sukkah is like one resting on the royal emblem; the forces of evil cannot act against him.

The Zohar (III, 217) is cited: when the destructive angel is asked "Where is the one you pursue?", he answers "I do not know" — because the person is hidden inside the Sukkah. Similarly, as long as the forces cannot see the person, they can do nothing to him.

This is the teaching of the Rebbe of Gur (the Chidushei haRim): the Zohar (III, 100) states that one who is not from the holy root of Israel cannot sit within the Sukkah under the shade of faith. The external forces — the angels of destruction — cannot even look in, blinded by the great sacred light within.

### Rabbi Avraham Yehoshua Heschel of Apt: The Hen and Her Chicks

The Apter Rov compares the role of the Shekhinah to a hen that first calls her chicks and sets food before them, and only afterward gathers them under her wings. So too: on Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur the Shekhinah inscribes for us food and life, and afterward takes us beneath her wings — this is the Sukkah.

Gematria is offered: the word **סֻכּוֹת** — first and last letters form **ס"ה** = 65, the numerical value of **אַדְנִ"י**, while the middle letters form **כ"ו** = 26, the value of the Tetragrammaton. The Sukkah is thus a vessel in which the Name **הֲוָיָ"ה** is clothed within **אַדְנִ"י**.

After Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur, we send a "gift" to the external forces (the scapegoat, שָׂעִיר לַעֲזָאזֵל) to silence their accusations. Afterward, we flee into the Sukkah — and the mechanism of this flight is our acceptance of the yoke of Heaven (קַבָּלַת עֹל מַלְכוּת שָׁמַיִם), crowning Hashem over every single limb, pledging that no movement of any limb will occur except according to the King's will. In this way we hide within the Kingdom of the King of Kings, and the Sukkah — whose letters encode the Divine Names — becomes the royal palace itself.

### Rabbi David of Dinov: Swaddled Like a Newborn

On Yom Kippur, Hashem forgives Israel and they become like a newborn infant. Just as a newborn is wrapped in garments so that the change in air does not harm it, so Hashem envelops Israel in the Sukkah — a garment of honor — as a parent shelters their child.

### Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Yeles: Running to the Mother's Apron

When a father wishes to punish his child, the child runs under the mother's apron. So too: after the Days of Judgment, Hashem commands us to sit in the Sukkah, which is the tent of our Mother (the supernal בִּינָה — אִמָּא עִלָּאָה) and shelter from all din. This is hinted in the verse (Genesis 18:6): **וַיְמַהֵר אַבְרָהָם הָאֹהֱלָה** — the righteous man hastens to the Tent (=the Sukkah, which is the shade of the Sukkah, shelter from judgment).

### Rabbi Yehudah Aryeh Leib of Gur: Protection from the Evil Eye of the Nations

After Israel emerges victorious and forgiven from Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur, the nations of the world wish to cast an evil eye upon them. Therefore Hashem shields them and hides them beneath His shade, so that the evil eye cannot rule over them.

---

## Part Two: The Sukkah as the Light of the Makifim — Gradual Revelation

### Rabbi Kalonymus Kalman Epstein of Kraków (Maor vaShemesh): The Sukkah as Tzimtzum of Light

The Midrash (Tanchuma, Vayakhel 6) teaches: "The Holy One, blessed be He, wrapped Himself [in light] and created the world." Hashem created the world so that His creatures could come to know His divinity. But the brilliance of His light is infinite — if a person were to perceive the full intensity of that light from the outset of their service, they could not withstand or contain it. Just as a person who has been in darkness for many days and then steps into full sunlight at noon would be blinded, so the soul must be acclimated gradually.

The Zohar (I, 170) explains why Isaiah (58:8) says **יִבָּקַע כַּשַּׁחַר אוֹרֶךְ** — "Your light will break forth like the dawn" — and not "like the sun": because the dawn light increases gradually, so that one can bear it.

After Yom Kippur, if a person were to receive the full intensity of divine light immediately, they could not bear it. Therefore Hashem commanded the mitzvah of Sukkah: we leave the house (the realm of the material) and enter the Sukkah — the **צִלָּא דִּמְהֵימָנוּתָא** — and the Imma Ila'ah (the supernal Mother, בִּינָה) hovers over us "as an eagle rouses its nest and hovers over its young" (Deuteronomy 32:11). The light is clothed in many garments and tzimtzumim. A person ascends from level to level, from holiness to holiness, until they can cleave to the Ein Sof.

This is what is meant by: Hashem "wrapped Himself and created the world" — He clothed His light in many garments of tzimtzum so that the creatures could bear it.

---

## Part Three: The Sukkah as Teshuvah — Continuation of Yom Kippur

### Rabbi Chaim of Sanz (Divrei Chaim): The Sukkah as Repair of What Was Damaged in the House

The Sukkah, being in the category of מַקִּיף (surrounding/encompassing light), alludes to teshuvah. The Gemara (Sukkah 26a) says **תֵּשְׁבוּ כְּעֵין תָּדוּרוּ** — "you shall dwell as you normally dwell." Whatever sins were committed in the ordinary course of sitting in the house — through eating, drinking, and the like — are now repaired through sitting in the Sukkah, through which we accept upon ourselves to improve all our ways.

### Rabbi Shlomo of Radomsk (Tiferet Shlomo): Clouds of Glory as Garments for the Soul

Israel left Egypt stripped of Torah and mitzvot, as the verse says (Ezekiel 16:7): **וְאַתְּ עֵרֹם וְעֶרְיָה** — "And you were naked and bare." The Clouds of Glory were the garments Hashem gave them — garments of honor — so that they would not be spiritually naked.

On Yom Kippur we do teshuvah and Hashem forgives us. But we still lack the "garment" — we have not yet accumulated sufficient Torah and mitzvot to clothe the soul. Therefore we are commanded to sit in the Sukkah, which is the surrounding light (אוֹר מַקִּיף) serving as a garment — so that we not remain spiritually naked.

This explains the piyyut: **אַשְׁרֵי אֱנוֹשׁ יַעֲשֶׂה זֹאת יֵחָשֵׁב לוֹ כְּשֹׁמֵר כָּל הַתּוֹרָה הַזֹּאת** — "Blessed is the person who does this — it shall be counted for him as if he kept all the Torah." By sitting in the Sukkah, the surrounding light becomes a garment for the soul as though he had fulfilled the entire Torah, from which the soul's garment is woven.

Similarly, the Yalkut Shimoni states: anyone who fulfills the mitzvah of Sukkah in this world — Hashem grants him a portion in the World to Come in the Sukkah of Leviathan. This means Hashem bequeaths to him meritorious deeds from others (similar to how Rabbi Elazar ben Durdaya received a garment from Yochanan the High Priest's accumulated mitzvot).

### Rabbi Yitzchak Meir of Gur (Chidushei haRim): Why We Don't Observe Sukkot in Nisan

The verse says (Leviticus 23:42-43): **לְמַעַן יֵדְעוּ דֹרֹתֵיכֶם** — "so that your generations shall know." Sukkah requires *da'at* (knowledge/consciousness). Therefore the Torah did not give us Sukkot in Nisan — because throughout the year we are filled with a spirit of folly (רוּחַ שְׁטוּת) and lack *da'at*. After Yom Kippur, when our sins are forgiven and we are "first in the accounting of sins" (רִאשׁוֹן לְחֶשְׁבּוֹן עֲוֹנוֹת), da'at returns to us — and then we can properly observe Sukkot.

The same Chidushei haRim explains: Sichon, king of Cheshbon, was the prince set over the attribute of *cheshbon hanefesh* (soul-accounting). While the Clouds of Glory surrounded Israel, this prince could not suppress their ability to make an inner accounting. But when the Clouds departed (after Aaron's death), Sichon attacked. Similarly, in ordinary times the external forces cover over the capacity for self-reckoning. Only during Sukkot, when the Clouds of Glory return, does the first day become **רִאשׁוֹן לְחֶשְׁבּוֹן עֲוֹנוֹת** — the first day of genuine soul-accounting.

---

## Part Four: The Sukkah as צִלָּא דִּמְהֵימָנוּתָא — Shelter of Faith

### Rabbi Gershon Henoch of Radzin: Hashem Desires That We Know He Protects Us

The verse (Leviticus 23:42-43): **לְמַעַן יֵדְעוּ דֹרֹתֵיכֶם כִּי בַסֻּכּוֹת הוֹשַׁבְתִּי** — "so that your generations shall know that I caused Bnei Yisrael to dwell in Sukkot when I took them out of Egypt." The Gemara (Sukkah 2a) rules: up to twenty cubits, a person knows they dwell in the shade of the Sukkah; above twenty cubits, the eye does not dominate (לֹא שָׁלְטָא בֵּיהּ עֵינָא) and one does not perceive it. The full desire of Hashem is that Israel recognize and feel that He is always protecting them.

### Rabbi David of Talna: Simple, Pure Faith Without Philosophical Investigation

The phrase **סֻכָּה תִּהְיֶה לְצֵל יוֹמָם** (Isaiah 4:6) — "A Sukkah shall be for shade by day" — alludes to the **צִלָּא דִּמְהֵימָנוּתָא**, the shelter of faith. The Sukkah teaches simple, complete faith: that the Creator created all the worlds and fills all existence. This faith must be **אֱמוּנָה פְּשׁוּטָה** — simple faith, stripping away all philosophical investigations and doubts. The Creator is utterly beyond all human comprehension (לֵית מַחֲשָׁבָה תְּפִיסָא בֵּיהּ כְּלָל — "no thought can grasp Him at all"). One who pursues philosophical doubts can fall into every evil. The way to arrive at true knowledge of G-dliness is precisely through first accepting simple faith — and from there, one merits a higher comprehension that transcends human grasp.

This is the meaning of **הַשְׂכֵּל וְיָדֹעַ אוֹתִי** (Jeremiah 9:23) — "Understand and know Me": first one must believe from the inner point of the heart, and only then does the higher השכלה (comprehension) descend from Above.

**לְמַעַן יֵדְעוּ דֹרֹתֵיכֶם** — "your generations" in plural, hinting from generation to generation until the generation of Mashiach — the faith must be transmitted as **אֱמוּנָה פְּשׁוּטָה** in every generation, until they merit that **מָלְאָה הָאָרֶץ דֵּעָה אֶת ה'** — "the earth shall be full of knowledge of Hashem" (Isaiah 11:9).

### Rabbi Yisrael of Padigura: Sukkah = Simple Faith; Before Repairing the Seven Midot

The Mishnah (Sukkah 28a): "All seven days a person makes his Sukkah his permanent dwelling and his house temporary." The Rebbe reads this as: **כָּל שִׁבְעַת הַיָּמִים** = during the entire time one is within the seven midot (character traits) and has not yet repaired them. **עוֹשֶׂה סֻכָּתוֹ קֶבַע** = the Sukkah (= simple faith) should be fixed and permanent for him. **וּבֵיתוֹ אַרְעֵי** = his "house" — meaning the intellect — should be considered temporary.

Just as at the Exodus from Egypt Israel was redeemed through simple faith (the Mechilta: **בִּזְכוּת הָאֱמוּנָה נִגְאֲלוּ** — "they were redeemed by merit of faith"), and only afterward, after repairing the seven midot through the seven weeks of the Omer, did they reach the level of receiving the Torah with da'at and intellect — so too the Sukkah teaches us to walk first in simple faith, and afterward merit faith through knowledge.

---

## Part Five: The Sukkah, Bitachon (Trust), and Prayer

### Rabbi Tzvi Hirsh of Rimnov (Be'ert haMayim): The Sukkah Symbolizes Bitachon

The verse (Genesis 33:17): **וְיַעֲקֹב נָסַע סֻכֹּתָה וַיִּבֶן לוֹ בַיִת וּלְמִקְנֵהוּ עָשָׂה סֻכֹּת** — "And Yaakov traveled to Sukkot and built himself a house, and for his cattle he made Sukkot." Why did Yaakov name the place after the Sukkot (booths for his cattle) and not after the house he built for himself?

Because the Sukkah alludes to **בִּטָחוֹן** (trust). One who sits in the Sukkah — exposed on all sides, with only a flimsy roof — demonstrates that his heart is steadfast and he fears no person. One who lacks trust hides in inner rooms behind iron doors. All of Yaakov's travels were to teach humankind the attribute of trust.

**וַיִּבֶן לוֹ בַיִת** — "He built himself a house": the word **בָּנָה** is related to **בִּינָה** (understanding) — he internalized the attribute of trust within himself. **וּלְמִקְנֵהוּ עָשָׂה סֻכֹּת** — he even taught his servants and all his acquisitions to have trust.

**Bitachon** comes from the root "plastering against a wall" — cleaving and adhering to Hashem. **עַל כֵּן קָרָא שֵׁם הַמָּקוֹם סֻכּוֹת** — therefore he called the *place* (= the Holy One, Who is the Place of the world) "Sukkot" — in prayer and supplication. One who has true trust can pray, because he has cast all his troubles far away.

---

## Part Six: The Sukkah's Sanctity — The Holy Names and Total Bodily Immersion

### Rabbi Yitzchak Eizik of Spinka (Chakal Yitzchak): The Only Mitzvah That Encompasses the Entire Body

All mitzvot sanctify the specific limb with which they are performed: tefillin sanctifies the hand and head; eating matzah sanctifies the mouth and esophagus. But no mitzvah encompasses the entire body — except the Sukkah, into which one enters with one's whole body and is thereby sanctified entirely.

This is why, according to the Gemara (Avodah Zarah 3a), when the nations in the future ask for a mitzvah to fulfill, Hashem offers them the "easy mitzvah" of Sukkah — because through it alone, the entire body can be sanctified at once.

The Divrei Chaim (Rabbi Chaim of Sanz) adds: even the Tallit with its tzitzit covers most of the body, but the feet do not enter it — whereas in the Sukkah even the feet enter. Moreover, the entire Sukkah is composed of Holy Names, and even the floor of the Sukkah has the presence of Holy Names — and Hashem commanded us to enter the Sukkah and literally walk upon those Names, to purify, sanctify, and atone for our sins.

From this derives the saying of the Chidushei haRim cited by his grandson: **אֵין לָנוּ מִצְוָה כָּזוֹ שֶׁנִּכְנָסִים בָּהּ עִם הַשְׁטִיוָועל** — "We have no other mitzvah that one enters with one's boots."

The word **עָקֵב** (heel) in the verse **וְהָיָה עֵקֶב** (Deuteronomy 7:12) alludes to the Sukkah: **עָקֵב** = two times the value of **סֻכָּה** (with two kollelim). The verse continues: **תִּשְׁמְעוּן אֶת הַמִּשְׁפָּטִים הָאֵלֶּה** (the laws of Sukkah), **וּשְׁמַרְתֶּם** (not eating outside), **וַעֲשִׂיתֶם אֹתָם** (eating, sleeping, sitting in the Sukkah).

### Rabbi Naftali of Ropshitz (Zera Kodesh): The Sukkah as Shadow of the Supernal Sukkah

Why is the person sitting under the schach called one dwelling in the **צֵל** (shadow/shade) of the Sukkah, rather than "under" it (as one would say under a tree)? Because the language of **צֵל** applies when something reflects or mirrors another thing at a distance — just as the Midrash says on the verse **ה' צִלְּךָ** (Psalms 121:5): "just as your shadow mirrors every movement you make, so everything a person does above stirs a corresponding response."

The Sukkah we build below is a **צֵל** (shadow/reflection) of the Supernal Sukkah — which is an utterly transcendent level emanated from the Supreme Emanator, with no separating screen (מָסָךְ) between it and the light of the Ein Sof. The Ein Sof illuminates within it so completely that the very distinction between them cannot be grasped. Our earthly Sukkah corresponds to this: just as the Sukkah's schach has no full ceiling separating it from the heavens (the stars and sky are visible through it), yet it is still distinct from the open sky — so it reflects the Supernal Sukkah's relationship to the infinite light.

The word **כּוֹכָב** (star) contains the letters **כ"ו כ"ב** = the Tetragrammaton (26) and the 22 letters of Torah — alluding to the fact that the mitzvah of Sukkah encompasses the entire Torah, and through it we cleave to Hashem.

---

## Part Seven: The Ushpizin (Supernal Guests)

### The Source and Nature of the Ushpizin

The Zohar (III, 103b) on the verse **בַּסֻּכֹּת תֵּשְׁבוּ שִׁבְעַת יָמִים** teaches that this alludes to the seven supernal shepherds — the Ushpizin — who dwell in the Sukkah when Israel fulfills the mitzvah. The verse uses **תֵּשְׁבוּ** (you shall dwell) for the Ushpizin and **יִשְׁבוּ** (they shall dwell) for the people — first the Ushpizin arrive, then Israel dwells.

The seven shepherds: **אַבְרָהָם, יִצְחָק, יַעֲקֹב, מֹשֶׁה, אַהֲרֹן, יוֹסֵף, דָּוִד**, corresponding to the seven midot:

| Day | Ushpiz | Sefirah |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Avraham Avinu | חֶסֶד |
| 2 | Yitzchak Avinu | גְּבוּרָה |
| 3 | Yaakov Avinu | תִּפְאֶרֶת |
| 4 | Moshe Rabbeinu | נֶצַח |
| 5 | Aharon haKohen | הוֹד |
| 6 | Yosef haTzaddik | יְסוֹד |
| 7 | David haMelech | מַלְכוּת |

The seven Ushpizin drew the faith in Hashem's G-dliness throughout the world — they crowned Him over every movement, every word, every thought.

### Why Sukkot Is Called Tzilla diMeheimanutah — Through the Ushpizin

The seven shepherds drew upon the world the **אֱמוּנַת אֱלֹקוּת** (faith in the Divine) — they achieved their level precisely because they clung to the attribute of **אֱמֶת** (truth) and distanced themselves from falsehood even by a hair's breadth, because as soon as a person departs from truth even a thread's width, they are immediately caught in falsehood (since the Kelipah always stands opposite the Kedushah).

### The Ushpizin as a Treasury of Merits for Israel

Rabbi Shaul Yedidyah of Modzitz offers a beautiful mashal: Israel is like a merchant who has accumulated debts to a great seller. On Erev Rosh Hashana, the great people of the city fast — a third of the debt is forgiven. During the Ten Days of Repentance — another third. On Yom Kippur — all of it. But how can Israel conduct business with Hashem again, having no spiritual currency to offer?

So Israel invites the Ushpizin — the patriarchs, the spiritual relatives — who gather their collective merits: Avraham contributes his חֶסֶד, Yitzchak his גְּבוּרָה, etc. Over the seven days of Sukkot, this treasury of merit accumulates, and then — immediately after Sukkot — we celebrate **שִׂמְחַת תּוֹרָה**. As the Rebbe of Kazmir put it in Yiddish: **"אוּן אַז מְ'הָאט זִיךְ שׁוֹין אָפְגֶערֶעכֶענְט, חוֹב מַאכְט מֶען זִיךְ שִׂמְחַת תּוֹרָה"** — "And once the debt has been settled, one makes Simchat Torah."

### How to Know the Ushpiz Has Arrived

Rabbi Yaakov Yakar of Yaet (Aron Edut) explains: you know the Ushpiz of each day has arrived when you feel that corresponding middah awakening within yourself. On the first day — if your heart burns with the חֶסֶד of Avraham, with desire to welcome guests and give charity — then Avraham has arrived. On the second day — if you feel an awakening of גְּבוּרָה to overcome your evil inclination — Yitzchak has arrived, and so on through the week.

### The Ushpizin Appear Through the Holy Midot — Divrei Chaim

The Tzanzer Rav teaches: the essential meaning of the Ushpizin is not primarily that the literal souls of the Patriarchs visit each Sukkah (though this is true). Rather, the Ushpizin represent their holy attributes, which shine within the Sukkah on each corresponding day. Each person should strive to embody the middah of that day's Ushpiz — fulfilling the Tanna's teaching (Tanna deVei Eliyahu 25): **מָתַי יַגִּיעוּ מַעֲשַׂי לְמַעֲשֵׂי אֲבוֹתַי** — "When will my deeds reach the level of my forefathers' deeds?"

---

## Part Eight: The Sukkah as Beit haMikdash and Eretz Yisrael

### Rabbi Yissachar Dov of Belz: The Air of the Sukkah is as Beit haMikdash and Eretz Yisrael

In the name of Rabbi Sar Shalom of Belz: the Sukkah, from all four sides and above and below, is in the holiness of the Beit haMikdash and Eretz Yisrael. Hence the prayer in the Siddur **"וּתְזַכֵּנִי לֵישֵׁב עַל אַדְמַת קֹדֶשׁ"** — "grant me to sit upon holy soil" — refers to the Sukkah itself, for the Sukkah's entire essence is the holiness of Eretz Yisrael.

The connection is through Yaakov Avinu — who uniquely drew the holiness of Eretz Yisrael to the diaspora. This is hinted in his dream of the ladder (Genesis 28:12), where the angels ascending and descending represented the transition between the two domains, and Eretz Yisrael's sanctity came to meet him even in chutz la'aretz. Hence the Sukkah — which corresponds to Yaakov — carries the holiness of Eretz Yisrael wherever one is.

### Sukkah = Sanctity of Korbanot

The Gemara (Sukkah 9a): just as the name of Heaven rests upon the festival offering (חֲגִיגָה), so it rests upon the Sukkah. Hence the Sukkah carries the holiness of the Korbanot and of Eretz Yisrael.

---

## Part Nine: The Sukkah as Mikveh — Purification

### Rabbi Avraham of Trisk (Magen Avraham): The Sukkah is a Mikveh for Israel

The mitzvah of Sukkah operates as a secret mikveh: "just as the mikveh purifies Israel before their Father in Heaven, so the Sukkah shields and saves them from all evils" (based on Isaiah 4:6: **וְסֻכָּה תִּהְיֶה לְצֵל יוֹמָם לַחֲסוֹת מִזֶּרֶם וּמִמָּטָר** — "A Sukkah shall be for shade by day, for shelter from storm and rain").

The word **סְכָךְ** in gematria equals **מֵאָה** (one hundred), and **מֵאָה** contains the letters **אִמָּא** (mother) — the secret of the Imma Ila'ah. Just as a frightened child hides under the mother's apron, so the Sukkah envelops all of Israel.

The Pri Etz Chaim and writings of the Baal Shem Tov explain: in the mikveh one must repair the seven midot, and the mikveh itself corresponds to the eighth level — Imma. So too the Sukkah: the four species correspond to the seven midot (חג"ת נהי"מ), and the Sukkah itself is the eighth — Imma Ila'ah. Therefore during Sukkot one should immerse in the mikveh every day, because their roots are one and the same.

---

## Part Ten: The Sukkah — Segulot and Spiritual Effects

### The Sukkah Repairs Adam's Sin of Improper Sight

Rabbi David of Talna: the word **סֻכָּה** is related to **סְתַּכְּלוּת** (gazing, looking). The Gemara (Megillah 14a) notes that Sarah was called **יִסְכָּה** because everyone gazed upon her beauty. All sins ultimately trace back to improper sight — **וַתֵּרֶא הָאִשָּׁה כִּי טוֹב הָעֵץ לְמַאֲכָל** (Genesis 3:6): "The woman saw that the tree was good to eat." By fulfilling the mitzvah of Sukkah — which is itself a form of elevated seeing — one repairs the faculty of sight and thereby repairs all the sins that stemmed from improper vision.

This also explains why a Sukkah higher than twenty cubits is invalid — because **לֹא שָׁלְטָא בֵּיהּ עֵינָא** (the eye does not govern/dominate it): the repair of sight requires that the eye be able to perceive and be governed by the Sukkah's holiness.

Through repairing the vision one merits supernal sight — **לַחֲזוֹת בְּנֹעַם ה' וּלְבַקֵּר בְּהֵיכָלוֹ** (Psalms 27:4): "To behold the pleasantness of Hashem and to visit in His Temple."

### The Sukkah Draws Ruach haKodesh

Rabbi Moshe Eliakim Briaa of Koznitz: **בַּסֻּכֹּת** means **seeing** and **listening** — from the root **סוֹכֶה** (to see with ruach hakodesh, as Sarah/Yiskah), and also from **הַסְכֵּת וּשְׁמַע** (give ear and hear). The numerical value of **סוּכָּ"ה** encodes the unification of **הֲוָיָ"ה** (26) and **אַדְנִ"י** (65). Through the Sukkah one can merit a glimpse of ruach hakodesh — but only proportional to how much one has created a dwelling for the Shekhinah within oneself throughout the year.

### The Sukkah as Segulah for Mental Settledness (Yishuv haDa'at)

Rabbi Ze'ev Wolf of Strikov: the Gemara (Sukkah 25a) states that even a mourner is obligated in the Sukkah — for it is upon him to settle his mind. From here we learn that through the mitzvah of Sukkah one can attain *yishuv hada'at* (mental settledness) for all divine service. Mitzvah leads to mitzvah — the Sukkah creates the composure needed for all the other mitzvot.

### The Sukkah Annuls Strange Thoughts

Rabbi Yerachmiel Yisrael Yitzchak of Alexander: the Sukkah neutralizes foreign thoughts and imaginings. The verse (Psalms 60:8): **וְעֵמֶק סֻכּוֹת אֲמַדֵּד** — Rashi explains **סֻכּוֹת** from the root of *dimyon* (imagination/fantasy). By entering the Sukkah — which is a mitzvah of מָקוֹם (place) just as Shabbat is a mitzvah of זְמַן (time) — and entering with the whole body, one sanctifies the entire spatial dimension and can neutralize the strange thoughts that arise from the imagination.

### The Sukkah as Gateway for Torah and Prayer

Rabbi Avraham Yissachar of Radomsk: the Sukkah is the gateway through which Torah and prayer ascend Above. The external forces (מַסְטִינִים) fill all the air of the world and obstruct the ascent of prayers. But Hashem gave us the Sukkah — which envelops and encompasses us — and within it, our Torah and prayers ascend directly. This is the meaning of **חַג הָאָסִיף** (the Festival of Ingathering): in this festival, all the Torah and mitzvot that scattered throughout the year and could not ascend are now "gathered in" and elevated. The word **מִזֶּרֶם** (from storm) in **מִסְתּוֹר מִזֶּרֶם** has the gematria of 248 (with the kollel) = the 248 positive commandments — the Sukkah protects all of them.

### The Sukkah Connects Us to the World to Come

Rabbi Natan of Breslov: after Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur, we sit in the Sukkah and rejoice with ultimate joy — because we now merit drawing the essence of **עוֹלָם הַבָּא** (the World to Come) into this world. The Sukkah is itself a manifestation of the World to Come within this world. This is the essential joy: experiencing the taste of the future world while still in this world.

---

## Part Eleven: The Sanctity of the Sukkah — Conduct Within

### The Ari (Rabbi Yitzchak Luria): Do Not Speak Idle Words in the Sukkah

The Ari's custom was not to speak any secular talk inside the Sukkah throughout the holiday. The reason: the Sukkah is filled with supreme holiness; the Shekhinah spreads its wings over us; the seven Ushpizin are present; the wood of the Sukkah is forbidden for benefit and has sanctity resting upon it (as the Gemara teaches, Sotah 9a); and the Zohar confirms that holy light of the Ushpizin's souls dwells there.

Rabbi Yonatan Eibschutz (Ya'arot Devash): one should multiply Torah study in the Sukkah, not gatherings for drinking and frivolity. The Sukkah is the **צִלָּא דִּמְהֵימָנוּתָא**, and the Torah is itself called a "shade of faith" — **בְּצִלּוֹ חִמַּדְתִּי וְיָשַׁבְתִּי** refers to the shade of Torah. To use the Sukkah for lewdness and sin would bring in the shadow of demons instead of the shadow of the Shekhinah.

### Lying in the Sukkah

The Sfat Emet (Rabbi Yehudah Aryeh Leib of Gur): the sanctity of the Sukkah reaches even the physical body — even when a person sleeps and the soul has temporarily departed, the Sukkah's holiness still affects the body (the *kista d'chayyuta*, the vital force). This is why the Mishnah mentions "sleepers beneath it" — to teach that the mitzvah affects even the body alone, in its purely physical state.

# Summary: Teachings on the Four Species (Arba'ah Minim) — Anthology from Chassidic Masters

## Overview

This text is a rich anthology of teachings, stories, and homiletical observations on the mitzvah of the Arba'ah Minim (the Four Species of Sukkot) drawn from dozens of Chassidic masters. The teachings span topics including: the proper intention (kavanah) in acquiring and taking the minim; their symbolic correspondences to the human body and to divine Names; the spiritual mechanics of the waving (naanuim); care against pride; the importance of beautification of mitzvot; warnings against hypocrisy in religious observance; and the deep connection between Sukkot and Yom Kippur.

---

## Section I: Acquiring the Four Species — Attitude and Intention

**R. Shmuel of Sokhatchov** (Shem MiShmuel) describes the custom of righteous men to purchase the etrog only *after* Yom Kippur, teaching that the quality of one's prayer and tears during the Yamim Nora'im determines the beauty of the etrog one merits. Those whose prayer was most powerful would unwrap their etrog — purchased sight-unseen — and find it perfectly unblemished. This is connected to the principle that one's tefillos and supplication, analogous to delicacies, earn one the merit of the Four Species; the shofar earns one the sukkah.

**R. Zeev Wolf of Zhitomir** (Ohr HaMeir) calls on anyone who wishes to approach the avodah of the Creator and to be among those who beautify the mitzvot to first examine himself carefully: is he truly worthy? What will his waving of the lulav bring about? Will it build or destroy? Every Jew must examine the innermost direction of his thoughts and intentions — from the moment of his acceptance of Malkhut Shamayim on Rosh Hashanah to the culminating act with the minim.

**R. Chaim Yosef David Azulai** (Moreh B'Etzba) states: one should not be miserly with expenditures for the Four Species and the sukkah. One spends freely on jewelry and clothing for one's household — how much more should one spend with great joy to obtain a beautiful etrog and a fine sukkah. To be stingy toward the mitzvot of Hashem is a source of shame.

**R. Shlomo of Radomsk** (Tiferet Shlomo) teaches that the excitement and eagerness with which one pursues the Four Species awakens a corresponding arousal on High — **ית'עוֹרְרוּתָא דִלְתַּתָּא** (arousal from below awakens an arousal above) — ascending all the way to the Infinite. When a person fears he will not be able to fulfill a beautiful mitzvah — an exquisite etrog, fine tefillin — and swears with longing to acquire them, the Holy One accounts that desire and oath as a meritorious deed on High.

**R. Natan of Breslov** (Likkutei Halakhot) teaches that when a person desires to perform a holy act, he cannot reach that holiness on his own — heaven sends him a hidden illumination from the root of that mitzvah's holiness to assist him. Specifically, when one yearns for a beautiful etrog but faces obstacles — poverty, unavailability — that very longing draws down light from the spiritual root of etrog holiness, which strengthens his will against the obstacles until he succeeds.

**R. Natan** also notes that money spent on mitzvot — Torah education for children, Shabbat, Yom Tov, tefillin, tzitzit, etrog, matzot — sanctifies all of Israel's wealth, giving it the status of **ממון דקדשה**, sacred funds.

**R. Chaim Meir of Vizhnitz** (Imrei Chaim) teaches that the word אתר"ג is a notarikon of the verse **אַל תְּבוֹאֵנִי רֶגֶל גַּאֲוָה** ("Let not the foot of pride come upon me," Tehillim 36:12). Those who are truly in awe of Hashem spend enormous sums on the Four Species and sukkah precisely to guard themselves from pride — knowing that all the honor belongs to Hashem.

**R. Nachman of Breslov** (Sichot HaRan) teaches that one who weeps more during the Yamim Nora'im merits a more beautiful etrog. The Zohar (Tikkunim 13) teaches that the etrog corresponds to **כֻּלָּךְ יָפָה רַעְיָתִי** ("You are completely beautiful, My beloved"), and Israel during the Yamim Nora'im are in the state of **"a crying child"** — inspiring immediate divine compassion. The degree of weeping corresponds to the beauty of the etrog one receives.

**R. Nachman** adds: the very fact that Israel spends more money on etrog than on almost any other mitzvah demonstrates its hidden greatness — Israel is wise and not easily fooled, so their spending is not in vain. The true depth of the etrog's spiritual value is beyond our comprehension.

**R. Yehoshua Heschel of Monastirische** (Erkei Yehoshua): he was particular to take only an etrog from Eretz Yisrael, citing his teacher R. Yochanan of Rachmistrivke, who said that when the Holy One commanded **וּלְקַחְתֶּם לָכֶם פְּרִי עֵץ הָדָר**, He certainly intended the etrog that grows in the Land of Israel.

---

## Section II: Warnings Against Hypocrisy and Hidden Pride

**R. Chaim of Tchernowitz** (Be'er Mayim Chayyim) offers a penetrating moral rebuke. There are those who are punctilious about public mitzvot — pursuing a beautiful etrog, a fine tallit, loud prayer with great kavanah — yet in the privacy of their homes they are careless about impure thoughts, doubtful prohibitions, and desires. Such a person is like one who is impeccably righteous in public but privately dissolute.

He identifies a subtle self-deception: a person's yetzer hara cleverly uses the desire for prestige to fuel outwardly pious behavior, while the person sincerely believes he is acting for the sake of Heaven. The proof: he does not pray as carefully when alone in a field, and does not pursue the finest etrog when traveling away from his community. If his intention were truly for Heaven, he would rejoice when his friend also obtained a beautiful etrog — but in fact he feels no joy in this.

He concludes: *"If a person does not fear Hashem in private as he does in public, then even what he does publicly is false."*

**R. Zeev Wolf of Zhitomir** (Ohr HaMeir) offers another teaching on the notarikon of etrog — **אַל תְּבוֹאֵנִי רֶגֶל גַּאֲוָה** — but directs it differently: many people buy the etrog while their hearts are completely distant from its true meaning. They are driven by vanity and prestige. The word **אֶתְרֹג** in this usage is written without the letter vav (as אתרג), which hints at the **הסתלקות** (withdrawal) of the Holy One, Who is symbolized by the vav. By contrast, those who acquire the Four Species to do the will of their Creator — wielding them like weapons of war — awaken the three Patriarchal lines of divine attributes, just as the shofar on Rosh Hashanah does.

---

## Section III: The Sanctity of the Four Species — Divine Presence in the Mitzvah Object

**R. Meshulam Feibush of Zbarizh** (Yosher Divrei Emet) cites the Midrash: **"The lulav — this is the Holy One; the etrog — this is the Holy One"** (Vayikra Rabbah 30:9). When we take these species in hand for the sake of Hashem's commandment, the אֱלֹהוּת that was previously hidden within every physical object becomes aroused and manifested — the divine power dwells upon it.

He uses the mashal: when someone takes a piece of silver or gold and engraves the king's seal upon it, that piece is elevated to a higher status — the king's name is called upon it. Similarly, when one takes an etrog for a mitzvah and directs his thought toward Hashem, the etrog is elevated and the Name of the King is called upon it. This is the meaning of **עוֹשֵׂי מִצְוָה** — the person is a מְתַקֵּן, one who perfects the object (etrog, lulavbranches, tzitzit threads) so that the King's seal is inscribed upon it.

**R. Tzvi Elimelech of Dinov** (Bnei Yissaskhar) teaches that performing a mitzvah **"beyond the letter of the law"** (be-yeter se'et u-bechumrot, with extra stringency and piety) illuminates the **אוֹר מַקִּיף** (surrounding/encompassing spiritual light), which has no defined vessel. This makif light drives away all accusers and the Sitra Achra, as the verse states: **"All the peoples of the earth shall see that the Name of Hashem is called upon you"** — meaning the makif light above the person. This is the inner meaning of **שְׁיָרֵי מִצְוָה מְעַכְּבִין אֶת הַפּוּרְעָנוּת** (remnants of mitzvah performance hold back punishment).

**R. Tzvi Elimelech** also notes (based on writing from the Arizal) that lulav + three bundles of hadasim gematrically equals **רָעָב = 272**, and when taken into the sukkah (whose schach = 100), the combined total equals **שֹׂבַע** (שבע, satiety = 372).

---

## Section IV: Symbolic Correspondences of the Four Species

| Species | Body Part | Spiritual Correspondence |
|---|---|---|
| Lulav | Spine (שִׁדְרָה) | Backbone/structure of person; leaves = brain above |
| Etrog | Heart (לֵב) | Seat of desire and will |
| Hadas | Eyes (עֵינַיִם) | Visual perception |
| Aravah | Lips/Mouth (שְׂפָתַיִם) | Speech |

This parallel (from Vayikra Rabbah 30:14 and multiple masters) teaches that taking the Four Species is an act of dedicating one's entire body — its four cardinal limbs — to the service of the Creator. **R. Naftali of Ropshitz** (Zera Kodesh) teaches that when taking them, one should intend to repair the damage done by the yetzer hara through each of these bodily organs, and to make oneself into a new being (**בְּרִיָּה חֲדָשָׁה**), dedicating these organs to the יֵצֶר טוֹב.

**R. Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk** (Pri HaAretz) teaches that the Four Species correspond to four gradations within all of Israel:
- Etrog: Torah and good deeds (taste and fragrance)
- Lulav: Torah without good deeds (taste without fragrance)
- Hadas: Good deeds without Torah (fragrance without taste)
- Aravah: Neither (neither taste nor fragrance)

What is true of the community is also true of every individual across time — there are periods of taste and fragrance, and periods of neither. The counsel in all states is simple **אֱמוּנָה** — faith that transcends intellect. This is hinted at in the binding: **"Your faithfulness surrounds You"** (Tehillim 89:9) — emunah encircles everything, even the highest levels.

---

## Section V: The Lulav Binding and Separation of the Species

**R. Shlomo of Karlin** (Midbar Kodesh): the custom of placing the hadasim and aravot inside leaf-pockets woven from the lulav itself (kaishielekh) comes from a mystical dream of the Belzer Rebbe, who saw that the divine Name הויה was being written with the final ה separated from the other letters. He protested and rewrote it whole. The lesson: the Four Species correspond to the four letters of the Name — they must neither be too far apart nor touching without space. The pockets maintain the proper divine configuration.

**The Chiddushei HaRim** (R. Yitzhak Meir of Gur) held the hadasim and aravot directly against the lulav without any separation, saying that this is like the דִּבּוּק (union/coupling) of the divine Name.

**R. Avraham of Slonim** (Beit Avraham) teaches that the binding symbolizes that even great spiritual figures need the level of **emunah peshutah** — simple, unadorned faith — as represented by the aravah (no taste, no fragrance). Even the Baal Shem Tov said: "After all my spiritual attainments, I stand and believe." The binding (agudah) hints at אמונה encircling all levels.

---

## Section VI: The Etrog's Separateness — The Tzaddik and the Community

**R. Menachem Mendel of Strizov** (Sheva Simachot) explains why the etrog is not bound with the other three species yet must be held together with them. The etrog corresponds to the tzaddik who has both Torah and mitzvot (taste and fragrance). The mashal: the tzaddik must connect himself with all the people in order to draw them to divine service — hence he holds the etrog together with the three other species during the mitzvah. But after the performance of the mitzvah, the etrog is separated and placed in a closed vessel, hinting that the tzaddik must afterward withdraw into privacy and hitbodedut (seclusion).

**R. Yosef of Papa** (Vayechi Yosef): the etrog is not bound with the others because the etrog corresponds to the heart (lev), and Rabban Yochanan ben Zakkai's disciples said the cardinal virtue is **לֵב טוֹב** — a good heart (Avot 2:9). The heart is the root of all good character traits; hence the etrog stands apart as the root/source.

---

## Section VII: The Naanuim — The Waving of the Lulav

This section contains some of the most profound Kabbalistic teachings in the entire text.

**R. Yosef Yoska of Dubna** (Yesod Yosef): the Four Species correspond to the four letters of the divine Name הויה. By waving them in the six directions, we demonstrate that Hashem rules over all six extremities of the world. Each time we bring them toward our heart, we demonstrate that we are internalizing the holiness acquired during the Yamim Nora'im — drawing the Name's kedushah into our hearts.

The two kavvanot of the waving:
1. We dedicate ourselves to the unification of the divine Name with all our hearts, just as we committed during the days of repentance
2. The heart is like the Ark containing the Torah Scroll — we prepare it to contain Yichud, kedushah, fear, and love of Hashem

**R. Menachem Nachum of Chernobyl** (Meor Einayim): the word **לולב** contains the letters **לב לו** — "his heart to Him." The holakhah (going out/extending) represents the subduing of the heart and life-force in returning to Hashem. The hava'ah (bringing back) represents the drawing down of divine abundance. The back-and-forth motion reflects the principle that perpetual pleasure ceases to be pleasure — Hashem withdraws the life-force, the person holds fast in his avodah, and then greater pleasure is felt upon its return.

Through the going-out (holakhah) the person submits to Hashem, and through the return (hava'ah) he draws the abundance of life-force and subjects all winds to the divine — this is how **מוֹלִיךְ וּמֵבִיא עֶלֶה וּמוֹרִיד** arrests evil winds and bad dews.

**R. Zeev Wolf of Zhitomir** (Ohr HaMeir) compares the waving to an infant swaddled in its mother's arms, making no movement except by her will. The waving instills the da'at received on Rosh Hashanah — the knowledge that in every direction (the six sefirot: chesed, gevurah, tiferet, netzach, hod, yesod) one moves only according to the divine will. The lulav with its species represents the seven days of Creation (the six extremities plus the central axis), and by waving it in all directions, one crowns Hashem's sovereignty in all six directions.

**R. Menachem Mendel of Lubavitch** (Tzemacha Tzedek / Ohr HaTorah) offers a profound Chabad teaching: the waving of the Four Species actualizes **bitul** (self-nullification) and draws down **da'at** (divine knowledge/connection) into the collective souls of Israel. Crucially, this effect comes specifically from physical species — the actual growing objects of this world. This is because the physical realm came directly from **Ein Sof** (the Infinite) through **sovev kol almin** (the light that encompasses all worlds), not through the chain of spiritual cause and effect (hishtalshelut). Therefore, a spiritual/mental act of bitul cannot achieve the same effect as physically waving the actual material species.

The lulav's four species are held with the kavvanah of drawing the flow (**hamshakhah**) from **Chokhma, Binah, Da'at** into the six middot — connecting the intellect to the heart.

**The Alter Rebbe / R. Shneur Zalman of Liadi** (Ohr HaTorah, Tanya school): each of the Four Species exhibits the quality of **yichud ve-hitkallelut** — unity and mutual inclusion — which is the precondition for the indwelling of divine light. The lulav's leaves unite along its spine; the hadas grows in threes on each stem (three-in-one); the aravah grows in brotherly companionship (acavah, brotherhood — related to the Gemara's explanation that they are called "aravah" because they grow together in unity). The etrog endures year-round without rotting precisely because it contains the quality of unity — it can tolerate extremes of cold and heat (contradictory forces), like one who has achieved bitul (self-nullification) and can therefore bear contradictions. The Second Temple was destroyed only because of baseless hatred (a lack of unity) — the root reason the divine light could not rest there.

**R. Naftali of Ropshitz** (Zera Kodesh): the kavvanah of the waving is to draw kedushah from the **moach** (brain/intellect) down to the **lev** (heart) and from the heart to the liver (which controls physical desires). The brain is spiritual, housing **binah**; the liver governs the lower organs and is the source of physical desires. By waving the lulav — which symbolizes the moach drawing down its flow to the lev, and from the lev to the liver — one subdues all the lower desires and subjects them to divine service. Similarly, the three directions of waving correspond to the three divine attributes (chesed/right, gevurah/left, tiferet/center), drawing good from each into the heart.

**R. Shlomo of Radomsk** (Tiferet Shlomo): the waving during **Hodu** at the beginning and end of Hallel corresponds to the awakening of the **Chasadim harishonim** — the primordial kindnesses that preceded the creation of the world. The verse **"Where are Your former kindnesses?"** (Tehillim 89:50) and **"The world was built with kindness"** (ibid. 89:3) indicate that the Hallel-naanuim draw down the divine kindnesses from the highest root: **ra'ava de-ra'avin** (the supreme divine will/pleasure that preceded Creation).

**R. Tzvi Hirsh of Riminov** (Be'arat HaMayim): the lulav corresponds to **the ladder of Ya'akov**. The lulav is the secret of the vav of the divine Name (vertically oriented, connecting heaven and earth). Its physical form (a palm branch) is rooted in earth, yet its kavanot reach the highest sacred worlds. The tzaddikim who perform the waving are like **malachei Elohim** — ascending to elevate holy sparks and descending to draw down divine abundance.

**R. Pinchas of Koritz** (Imrei Pinchas): all the winds that blow in the world are generated by the swaying movements of Israel as they engage in Torah study. Furthermore, the word **נָא** (na — meaning "please," found in הוֹשַׁעְנָא and הוֹשִׁיעָה נָא) also means "waving" — it is itself a form of the naanua, a verbal wave.

Each direction of waving carries its own meaning:
- **North**: We pray that Israel's holiness be preserved and that holy souls be born in the world
- **East**: We pray for the light of Hashem to shine in every Jewish heart (שֶׁמֶשׁ is the sun, corresponding to Havayah — "For a sun and shield is Hashem Elohim")
- **Up**: Elevation of sparks from the lowest level to the highest
- **Down**: Drawing divine abundance from above to below
- **South/Right**: Drawing down the attribute of Tiferet/Chesed to "pass over transgressions" — transforming **פֶּשַׁע** (transgression) to **שֶׁפַע** (abundance)
- **West**: Gathering scattered holy sparks

**R. Yisrael Dov of Yasnitsha** (Revid HaZahav): by waving in all four directions we atone for sins committed in each direction (north, south, east, west), just as the walls of the Beit HaMikdash would atone for sins corresponding to each direction. Each direction awakens a particular tendency: north — greed (as in "from the north gold comes," Iyov 37:22); east — inappropriate love. By holding the divine Name in our hands and waving, we repair all of these.

**R. Menachem Mendel of Strizov** also teaches: the waving elevates all mitzvot performed without proper kavvanah during the year, drawing **mochin** (intellectual/spiritual illumination) down from Chokhma, Binah, Da'at into the mitzvot retroactively.

---

## Section VIII: The Sukkah and the Taking of the Lulav Inside It

**R. Shlomo Teomin** (Ohr HaTorah): taking the lulav inside the sukkah corresponds to the verse **"The Tree of Life in the midst of the garden"** — the lulav is the Tree of Life, and the sukkah is the garden (gan). The inner meaning: the essential fulfillment of the Four Species is specifically within the sukkah.

**R. Avraham of Pokhchuv**: the sukkah corresponds to the Beit HaMikdash (as the Gemara states, **שֵׁם שָׁמַיִם חָל עַל הַסֻּכָּה** — the divine Name rests upon the sukkah as upon the Temple). The Four Species correspond to the human limbs. When one takes the species inside the sukkah, one sanctifies all one's limbs and thereby draws the Shekhinah to dwell within oneself — **וְשָׁכַנְתִּי בְּתוֹכָם**, "And I will dwell within them" (the true meaning: within each individual who makes himself a mishkan).

**R. Yekutiel Yehuda of Sighet** (Yeitav Panim): the taking of the lulav in the sukkah represents the interplay of **ratzo va-shov** (running-toward and running-back) — the person draws near to Hashem with love and joy, then pulls back in awe and fear lest he dissolve in the divine light (kilot hanefesh). The waving toward each direction and the rule not to touch the wall with the lulav express this dynamic: draw near out of love, but do not touch lest you be annihilated — immediately return in reverence.

---

## Section IX: Specific Halakhic Practices and Their Deeper Meaning

**The Stolen or Dried Lulav** (R. Shlomo of Radomsk, Tiferet Shlomo): the Mishnah states that a stolen (gazul) or dried (yaresh) lulav is invalid. On the deeper level: if a person performs mitzvot without prior teshuvah, his sin has given the Sitra Achra power, and his mitzvot literally fall into the hands of the Chitzonim (external forces) — this is the true meaning of **"mitzvah committed through transgression."** The stolen lulav hints that one's mitzvah has been "stolen" by the Sitra Achra. The halachic requirement **"yours — not stolen"** is a Torah instruction that the fruits of your mitzvot should remain yours.

**Lulav on Shabbat** (R. Tzvi Elimelech of Dinov, Bnei Yissaskhar): the Rabbis enacted that certain mitzvot (shofar, lulav, megillah) are not performed on Shabbat. The inner reason: the spiritual tikkunim accomplished by these mitzvot are also accomplished by the holiness of Shabbat itself. The three names of Shabbat (Shabbat, Kallah, Malkeita) hint at the spiritual roles of shofar, lulav, and megillah respectively. Furthermore, the initials of **וַיָּבֹא יַעֲקֹב שָׁלֵם** hint at Shofar, Lulav, Megillah, and עיר שכם hints at the three names of Shabbat.

**R. Shmuel of Sokhatchov**: there is a concern that on Shabbat, if the lulav were to be taken, da'at (knowledge/consciousness) would be doubled — because Shabbat itself is da'at (**"to know that I, Hashem, sanctify you"**) — and this surplus of da'at would overflow the vessels, potentially leading to **"his wisdom exceeding his deeds."**

**R. Yisrael of Kozhnitz** (Avodat Yisrael) explains at length that the naanua (waving) represents the union (**zivug/yichud**) of Tzaddik and Malkhut. The holakhah (extension outward) symbolizes the temporary separation needed before the deeper union of the hava'ah (return). On Shabbat, when the yichud happens automatically by virtue of the day's holiness, it would be inappropriate to perform the holakhah — which would symbolize a separation — as this would amount to a **seter** (dismantling) that is not "for the purpose of building," since the building is already complete on Shabbat.

---

## Section X: The Etrog — Deeper Teachings

**R. Yaakov Abuchatzeira** (Pituchei Chotam): the word etrog with its gematriah (including two kollelim) equals **ברית** (brit). The etrog is called **פְּרִי עֵץ הָדָר** — where **פְּרִי** hints at procreation (fruitfulness), **עֵץ** refers to the body of the person, and **הָדָר** means it must be pure and beautiful. The entire mitzvah of the Four Species is a tikun for the body's sacred faculties: the lulav = heart (and all its thoughts bound to divine service, like the palm in its devotion); hadas = eyes (to be guarded from seeing evil); aravah = mouth/lips (to be guarded from improper speech). He develops each species in intricate detail as a code for bodily purity.

**R. Avraham of Trisk** (Magen Avraham): the phrase **פְּרִי עֵץ הָדָר** alludes to:
- **פרי**: gematria of **רץ** ("running") — alluding to Avraham and the principle from Sefer Yetzirah "if your heart runs, return to One"
- **כַּפּוֹת תְּמָרִים**: subduing the world of change and transience (the material world)
- **עֲנַף עֵץ עָבֹת**: seeking counsel throughout one's life, even into old age, to bend one's nature to divine service
- **עַרְבֵי נַחַל**: the sweetness derived from recognizing one's lowliness — that all one has comes from the זְכוּת אָבוֹת (merit of the patriarchs), not one's own accomplishment

**R. Natan of Breslov** (Likkutei Halakhot): the etrog grows on a tree where the **taste of the wood and the taste of the fruit are equal** (Sukkah 35a). This hints at the spiritual level of experiencing the World to Come within this world — feeling the fruit's taste within the tree itself, i.e., experiencing olam haba even while doing mitzvot in this world.

**R. Menashe of Worki**: the lulav is held in the right hand and the etrog in the left, since the lulav hints at **life** (לולב in gematria = חיים), and the etrog hints at **livelihood** (parnasah). Right corresponds to chesed and life; left corresponds to material sustenance.

**R. Shlomo of Chelm** (Divrei Shlomo of Tshortkov): since the Four Species hint at the unity of Israel, only one etrog is taken, representing the highest class that must be most unified. Multiple hadasim and aravot are permitted because the lower classes may be more splintered.

---

## Section XI: The Four Species as Weapons of War

**R. Yisrael of Kozhnitz** (Ohr Yisrael): when Hashem takes pride in Israel as they hold the Four Species, He can, as it were, fight on behalf of Israel against those who would seize their divine abundance. This is the meaning of **מָאנֵי קְרָבָא** (weapons of war) — as the Zohar states: **"Who won the judgment? He who holds the weapons of war in his hands."**

# שִׂמְחַת בֵּית הַשׁוֹאֵבָה — Simchat Beit HaSho'evah & Hoshana Rabbah
## Detailed Summary of a Chassidic/Kabbalistic Anthology

---

## Overview

This text is an anthology of teachings from dozens of Chassidic masters on two interconnected themes: **Simchat Beit HaSho'evah** (the Water-Drawing Celebration of Sukkot) and **Hoshana Rabbah** (the seventh day of Sukkot and the final sealing of the divine judgment). The material is organized as a florilegium — each section bears the name of a rebbe and a thematic heading — drawn from a wide range of Chassidic schools (Breslov, Chabad, Gerrer, Aleksander, Vizhnitz, Satmar, Munkatch, and many others). The central questions running through both sections are: What is the essential nature of this joy? What does it repair? And why does the Sukkot season function as the culmination of the entire High Holiday process?

---

## Part One: Simchat Beit HaSho'evah

### 1. The Name and Its Inner Meaning

The Talmud (Yerushalmi, Sukkah 5:1) explains that the Beit HaSho'evah — the "House of Drawing" — was so named because from there they would draw רוּחַ הַקֹדֶשׁ (the divine spirit). The surrounding texts explore what this means in every generation.

The opening citation, attributed to the Chatam Sofer (Rabbi Moshe Sofer), interprets the verse **"וּשְׁאַבְתֶּם מַיִם בְּשָׂשׂוֹן"** ("You shall draw water with joy," Isaiah 12:3) by distinguishing שִׂמְחָה from שָׂשׂוֹן:

- **שִׂמְחָה** (simchah) is an inner joy contained within a vessel, proportioned to one's capacity.
- **שָׂשׂוֹן** (sason) is joy that overflows the vessel's form entirely.
- **מַיִם** (water) has no fixed shape or color — it too is beyond the vessel.
- **תְּשׁוּבָה** (repentance) likewise transcends natural limits.

Therefore the verse links drawing water with sason: both water and genuine repentance exist in a mode that transcends the natural contours of the self.

---

### 2. The Joy in the Present Day — Faith as the Replacement for Prophecy

**Rabbi Yisrael of Padigura** distinguishes two historical eras:

- **During the Beit HaMikdash**: The joy was over the actual drawing of רוּחַ הַקֹדֶשׁ — for prophecy was present.
- **In our time**: The joy is an act of **אֱמוּנָה** (faith) — we believe that the Temple will be rebuilt and that the drawing of רוּחַ הַקֹדֶשׁ will return. This emunah is itself a point of inner life (נְקֵדָּה פְּנִימִית שֶׁל הַלֵּב — an inner point in the heart), and from this point everything flows.

---

### 3. The Joy Was Purely Spiritual — Not from Wine

**Rabbi Chaim Meir of Vizhnitz** emphasizes: the water libation was from water, not wine. Therefore the joy could not have been from intoxication. It was purely joy in the Almighty Himself — **שָׂמְחוּ בַּשֵׁם יִתְבָּרַךְ**. This stands in stark contrast to frivolous celebration.

**The Ohr HaMeir (Rabbi Zeev Wolf of Zhitomir)** adds a sharp warning: if the simchah is not purely spiritual — if it is generated by eating, drinking, and fine clothes — then it is not the simchah of Beit HaSho'evah at all. Worse, it risks violating the principle **"כִּי בְשִׂמְחָה תֵצֵאוּ"** (Isaiah 55:12) — that impure joy can carry a person outside the boundary of holiness. He states explicitly: such joy makes the Satan dance, and produces only frivolity, lewdness, and strife.

---

### 4. Joy, Forgiveness, and the Sequence of the Yamim Noraim

**The Lachazot Beno'am (Rabbi Avraham of Slotzkov)** develops a key theological arc connecting the Yamim Noraim to Sukkot:

The Tur (Orach Chaim 581) cites a midrashic contrast: when the nations go to judgment they wear black and are uncertain of the verdict; but Israel dresses in white and eats and rejoices on Rosh Hashanah, because they know God will perform a miracle for them. This confidence itself generates the merit to be acquitted.

The mechanics of forgiveness are then traced through several sources:

- Midrash (Bamidbar Rabbah 21:21): No one slept in Jerusalem with sin on their hands — the morning tamid atoned for the night's sins, and the afternoon tamid for the day's.
- Gemara (Yoma 39b) in the name of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai: The Temple is called "Levanon" because it whitens Israel's sins like snow, and because all hearts rejoice in it — hence Jerusalem is called "משׂוֹשׂ כָּל הָאָרֶץ" ("the joy of all the earth," Psalms 48:3).

**The structure of the joy:**
- On Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, the simchah is hidden (כִּמְסְתָּר): the angels themselves ask why Israel sings no שִׁירָה when the King sits in judgment with the books of life and death open. Answer: it would be unseemly.
- On Sukkot, the joy is revealed (בְּגָלוּי): the Torah commands it — "זְמַן שִׂמְחָתֵנוּ."
- The final sealing of judgment on Hoshana Rabbah occurs precisely when joy is commanded. A Jew who trusts that his sins have been forgiven, and celebrates from that trust, thereby actualizes his acquittal.

This is why the custom developed to sing the Yom Kippur piyutim (liturgical poems) on Sukkot nights — but now with joy rather than trembling. On Yom Kippur we sang them in fear; now, in the joy of Sukkot, we sing them in exaltation, because we believe we have been forgiven.

---

### 5. Nisuch HaMayim (the Water Libation) — Its Inner Meaning

The text dedicates extended attention to the spiritual meaning of the water libation. Several masters elaborate:

**The Bnei Yissaschar (Rabbi Tzvi Elimelech of Dinov)** teaches: water is the element from which all loves and pleasures flow. Sefer Sha'arei Kedushah (Rabbi Chaim Vital) states that from the element of water within a human being flows the love of pleasures — water nourishes all kinds of delight. Therefore, pouring water before Hashem on Sukkot is a declaration: we nullify all lower loves and pleasures against the love of God. The "great repair" (תִּקוּן גָּדוֹל) performed in the Women's Courtyard was specifically repair of the aspect of חֶסֶד and לוּלָב (the sefirah of Chesed/Gedullah), rectifying the damage caused by sexual sin (עֲוֹן הַזְנוּת), which comes through levity of the heart (זַחוּת הַלֵּב).

**The Peleh Yo'etz (Rabbi Mordechai Dov of Hornostypol)** offers the most systematic treatment:

The purpose of the water libation was to reveal and illuminate within כְּנֶסֶת יִשְׂרָאֵל the aspect of **תַּעֲנוּג** (delight) in the service of God. He distinguishes two types of pleasure using a parable:

| Type of Pleasure | Analogy | Spiritual Parallel |
|---|---|---|
| **Anticipatory pleasure** (before the act) | A person plans a beautiful house; pleasure arises in the mind before building | Joy before performing a mitzvah — "שָׂשִׂים וּשְׂמֵחִים לַעֲשׂוֹת רְצוֹן קוֹנָם" |
| **Fulfillment pleasure** (after completion) | When the house is finished and he enters, he experiences the pleasure he originally imagined | Joy during the performance of the mitzvah — "עִבְדוּ אֶת ה' בְּשִׂמְחָה" (Psalms 100:2) |

The second pleasure is called **אוֹר חוֹזֵר** (reflected light) — it returns to and completes the original anticipatory pleasure.

The High Holiday season operates via **גְבוּרוֹת** (severity/bitterness): during the Ten Days of Repentance, each person feels the bitterness (מְרִירוּת) of having sinned. This bitterness draws from the sefirot of Gevurot. From this bitterness arises a burning longing (שְׁתַּדְּבֵּק בִּדְרָכָיו יִתְבָּרַךְ) to cleave to God's ways.

Then on Sukkot — after Yom Kippur, when the Divine will is revealed and sins are forgiven — the dimension of **חֲסָדִים** (lovingkindnesses) and inner delight begins to awaken. The Sukkot joy is specifically the joy over the prospect of being included in the light of God (**הִתְכַּלְלוּת בְּאוֹר ה'**) and united with Him — a union fully realized only on Shemini Atzeret.

The drawing of water (שְׁאִיבָה) was during the preparatory phase — not the actual mitzvah — yet the joy was aroused precisely then. This shows that the joy of Sukkot is a **מַקִיף** (encompassing light), not dependent on the details of any particular act, but encompassing everything at once.

**Why only the Chassidim and Anshei Ma'aseh danced:** Because only they had sufficiently purified hearts to experience this inner joy visibly. Yet all the people watched — because every person can arouse in their mind and thought at least the anticipatory simchah: joy over the fact that they will merit to do the will of their Creator and be included in God's light.

---

### 6. Encouragement for the Simple — The Water Libation as Sign

**The Lachazot Beno'am (Rabbi Avraham of Slotzkov)** draws encouragement for ordinary Jews from the water libation itself: the water used required no special preparation — just clean spring water was sufficient. This teaches that even those who did not achieve great elevation during the Yamim Noraim can still approach God, wherever they are, and be received.

---

### 7. Simchat Beit HaSho'evah in the Women's Courtyard — the Rectification of Forgetting

**Rabbi Avraham of Trisk** gives a unique interpretation of why the celebration took place specifically in the **עֶזְרַת נָשִׁים** (Women's Courtyard). The Zohar (1:170b) teaches that "nashim" (women) can also be read as "nishiyah" — forgetting. Those Jews who have fallen into a mode of forgetfulness of their constant obligation to serve the Creator have descended into **עָלְמָא דְּפֵרוּדָא** (the world of separation). The celebration in the Women's Courtyard was designed to give them assistance (עֵזֶר) and raise them back to holiness. The "great repair" was specifically about bringing these souls back into connection with their Creator.

---

### 8. "No Sleep in Our Eyes" — The Continuous Avodah

The Gemara (Sukkah 53a) records that the great Sages proclaimed: "We have not seen sleep in our eyes" throughout the Simchat Beit HaSho'evah. The text brings several interpretations:

**The Tiferet Shlomo (Rabbi Shlomo of Radomsk):** The statement means not that they did not sleep but that they saw no **שִׁינּוּי** (change/deterioration) in each other's avodah. They supported one another (מִכַּתְפֵי אַהֲדָדֵי — literally "resting on each other's shoulders") so that when one fell into sadness or depression about his lack of achievement, his friend would support him. This mutual support (אַהֲבַת חֲבֵרִים) is what kept their avodah seamless.

**The Divrei Binah (Rabbi Yitzchak Yaakov of Biala)** takes this further based on a teaching from the Yehudi HaKadosh: when one person slept, his friend literally carried on his avodah for him — so that in truth their service was never interrupted even for a moment. This is the meaning of "they did not see sleep in their eyes" — because their avodah was never broken.

**Baruch Moshe (Rabbi Moshe of Satmar):** All of Israel was connected shoulder to shoulder, reaching upward all the way to the אָבוֹת (the Patriarchs) — a chain of connection that extended to Avraham, Yitzchak, and Yaakov. Through this chain they achieved a level of avodah so elevated that even their physical needs (eating, drinking, sleeping) were served by this holy labor.

---

### 9. The Woman Sifting Wheat — Repairing Adam's Sin

The Gemara (Sukkah 53a) records: a woman was sifting wheat by the light of the Beit HaSho'evah. One interpretation (attributed to the father of the Yissa Brachah, citing Sanhedrin 70a where the Tree of Adam is identified with wheat): on this night, when they merited the great lights, they searched through layer upon layer to find the root of all sins, reaching all the way to the sin of Adam HaRishon and working to repair it. The woman "sifting wheat" is a metaphor for this spiritual investigation and rectification.

**Rabbi Avraham of Slonim** connects this to the idea that on Sukkot, after the repair of sins, those present were in such a complete state of mind (דַּעַת שְׁלֵמָה) that even a woman could sift her speech to speak only with wisdom and knowledge. He notes that חִטָּה (wheat) has a gematria of 22, corresponding to the 22 letters of the Hebrew aleph-bet that constitute all speech.

---

### 10. Why "Beit" (House) — Permanence of the Joy

The Rebbe Maharash (Lubavitcher Rebbe, Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Lubavitch) asks: the celebration was not in any particular house — it was in the Women's Courtyard, which was outdoors. Why is it called Simchat **Beit** HaSho'evah?

His answer: the word "beit" signals **permanence** (קְבִיעוּת). Although the celebration was only for the seven days of Sukkot, its impact was so lasting that the place itself was named after it. Moreover, through this celebration the joy spread — in a permanent, established mode — to all of Israel's communities throughout the world, not only in Jerusalem. The use of "beit" reflects this quality of rootedness and endurance that the joy created.

---

### 11. "If I Am Here, All Is Here" — No One Is Excluded

**Rabbi Yitzchak of Amshinov** cites the famous statement of Hillel (Sukkah 53a): when he was rejoicing at the Simchat Beit HaSho'evah, he would say: **"אִם אֲנִי כָּאן הַכֹּל כָּאן"** — "If I am here, all is here."

The interpretation offered: just as the halachah follows Hillel in all cases — meaning no one can exempt themselves from Hillel's rulings — so too no one can exempt themselves from the Simchat Beit HaSho'evah. It is relevant to every single person. Just as there is no one so exalted that Hillel's halachah does not apply to them, there is no one so elevated or so lowly that the Simchat Beit HaSho'evah does not touch them. The celebration and its drawing of holy energy is available to every Jew.

---

### 12. The Dance of the Chassidim and Anshei Ma'aseh — A Study in Humility and Rising

**The Yeitav Panim (Rabbi Yekutiel Yehuda of Sigot)** gives a careful reading of the Mishnah's description: **"חֲסִידִים וְאַנְשֵׁי מַעֲשֶׂה הָיוּ מְרַקְדִים לִפְנֵיהֶם אֲבוּקוֹת שֶׁל אוֹר שֶׁבִּידֵיהֶן"** — "The Chassidim and Anshei Ma'aseh would dance before them with torches of fire in their hands."

- **חֲסִידִים** refers to those whose primary avodah is inward — the heart, intention (כַּוָּנָה) — like waves on the sea that constantly strive upward (drawing on Zohar 1:69b on "בְּשׂוֹא גַלָּיו תְּשַׁבְּחֵם" — the waves' striving upward is itself a form of praise, even if they fall back down). Even when they fall, the desire to rise is precious in God's eyes.
- **אַנְשֵׁי מַעֲשֶׂה** refers to those who cannot yet attain heart-level avodah, but who dance — striving to rise beyond their current level. They do this **בַּאֲבוּקוֹת** (with torches, i.e., the Torah and mitzvot in their hands), longing through the act of the mitzvah to eventually reach the level of inner avodah.

The Zohar (2:93b) is cited: "Blessed is one who performs his Master's commandments, even if his intention was not as it should have been" — provided his soul longs to reach beyond.

**The Shaar Yissachar (Rabbi Chaim Elazar of Munkatch)** adds: the dance itself symbolizes the soul's perpetual **עֲלִיָּה וִירִידָה** (ascent and descent) in divine service. A dancer goes up and comes back down, then rises again. This is also the karban oleh v'yored (the ascending-and-descending offering) mentioned in Vayikra — a hint that even the person who repeatedly rises and falls in avodah is acceptable before God, so long as they continue to rise.

The practical upshot: one should not become arrogant over one's good deeds (lest pride cause downfall) nor despondent over one's sins (lest depression extinguish joy entirely). Rather, hold both in balance — a person is always half innocent, half liable (Kiddushin 40b) — and use the joy of the fact that one may yet do God's will as the engine of renewal.

---

### 13. The Torches — Four or Eight?

**The Rimzei Chochmah (Rabbi Shmuel Leib Zhak)** notes that some danced with four torches and others with eight. The deeper meaning:

- **Four torches**: the four letters of the divine Name הויה.
- **Eight torches**: the eight letters of the combined divine Names הויה and אדני.

The joy came from the **בֵּירוּרִים** (sifting of divine sparks from the kelipot) accomplished through the Torah and mitzvot of the preceding year — "just as [it is said]: happy is one who arrives here with his Torah in his hand" (Bava Batra 10b).

---

### 14. The Prostrations — Humility at the Height of Attainment

**The Ahavat Shalom (Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Kosov)** records that those celebrating Simchat Beit HaSho'evah would also prostrate themselves (מִשְׁתַּחֲוִים) repeatedly. The explanation: the greater the spiritual attainment (שָׂגָה), the deeper the accompanying humility (שִׁפְלוּת). The prostrations reached the level of **פִּשׁוּט יָדַיִם וְרַגְלַיִם** — full stretching of hands and feet — which represents the greatest possible self-nullification.

He identifies three levels of humility corresponding to three archetypal figures (the acronym **אמ"ת** — truth):
- **א** — אֵ'פֶר (ash) = Avraham: "וְאָנֹכִי עָפָר וָאֵפֶר" ("I am dust and ash," Genesis 18:27)
- **מ** — מָ'ה (what/nothing) = Moshe: "וְנַחְנוּ מָה" ("And we are what?" Exodus 16:7)
- **ת** — תּוֹ'לַעַת (worm) = David: "וְאָנֹכִי תוֹלַעַת" ("I am a worm," Psalms 22:7)

---

### 15. Good Eye and the Joy of Giving

**The Beit Yaakov (Rabbi Yaakov of Izhbitsa)** makes a remarkable observation: in the entire Talmudic description of the Simchat Beit HaSho'evah, no food or drink is mentioned — only the light and the togetherness (צַוְתָּא). True joy, he says, comes from togetherness and from the divine light shining within a person to illuminate their relationship with their Creator. God created human beings so that each one should receive from the other. The exile itself is caused by **צְרַת עַיִן** (constricted eye/stinginess). At the Simchat Beit HaSho'evah, each person brought out the goodness they had accumulated in their service of God and gave it to the other freely.

**Why it is called after the Drawing, not the Pouring:** The mitzvah was the actual nisuch (pouring). Yet the celebration is named after the she'ivah (drawing/fetching). He explains: Sukkot corresponds to Yaakov — the עַמּוּדָא דְּאֶמְצָעִיתָא (the central pillar) who connects human effort with the divine will. The mikveh works by the person nullifying themselves completely; yet drawn water (מַיִם שְׁאוּבִין) invalidates a mikveh because it reflects human action rather than divine encompassing. Here on Sukkot, the mitzvah is precisely to elevate human effort (פְּעֶלַת הָאָדָם) into the divine sphere — hence the she'ivah (human drawing) is what is celebrated.

---

## Part Two: Hoshana Rabbah

### Overview

The text then turns to Hoshana Rabbah — the seventh day of Sukkot, day of King David's ushpiza, when the final sealing of the judgment is concluded and the pitka (divine decree note) is issued.

---

### 16. The Name "Hoshana Rabbah" — Multiple Interpretations

The anthology brings an extraordinary range of explanations for the name:

| Source | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Rabbi Shalom of Brahin | "Hoshana Rabbah" = great salvation in both physical and spiritual dimensions |
| Rabbi Aharon of Zhitomir | "Hoshana Rabbah" = great chasadim and complete rachamim, because Israel performed so many mitzvot from Rosh Hashanah to this day |
| Rabbi Tzvi Elimelech of Dinov | "Hoshana Na" — save us on the 51st day counting from Rosh Chodesh Elul; "Hoshana Rabbah" = the 51st day = the day of the final sealing |
| Rabbi Tzadok HaKohen of Lublin | The name refers to the great cry in the heart — Hoshana Rabbah is the day when the most intense cry for salvation wells up within every Jewish heart |
| Rabbi Avraham Mordechai of Ger | Even those who were not saved on Yom Kippur can be saved on Hoshana Rabbah — it is a "great salvation" open to all |
| Rabbi Yoel of Satmar | "Davar gadol" (a great matter) = Ma'aseh Merkavah — on this day we pray specifically for the glory of the Shechina and divine Kingdom, which is the "great thing" (as opposed to halachic dialectics = davar katan) |
| Rabbi Menashe of Vorki | Acronym: ר' of רַבָּה = life/health; ה' = blessings/children; ב' = children; א' = sustenance — all three of the bnei chayei umzonei |
| Rabbi Yitzchak of Amshinov | God, like a loving father, wants to give His children every opportunity to merit in judgment, so He extends the time repeatedly, through Chanukah, Purim, and beyond |

---

### 17. The Shadow Sign — The Ramban and the Rema

**The Lachazot Beno'am** brings the famous tradition about the shadow seen in moonlight on Hoshana Rabbah night: according to the Abarbanel and the Ramban (on Bamidbar 14:9), someone whose head casts no shadow in the moonlight on this night has been decreed to die in the coming year ("סָר צִלָּם מֵעֲלֵיהֶם" — "their shadow has departed from them").

The Rema (Orach Chaim 664:1) mentions this but then says it is better to be simple-hearted (תָּמִים) and not to scrutinize these signs.

The Lachazot Beno'am interprets the Rema's intent not as dismissal but as **praise of Israel's simple faith**: because the Jewish people trust God and don't fixate on negative omens, that very trust functions to transform any potentially negative sign into a positive outcome. Furthermore, following the Baal Shem Tov's interpretation of "ה' צִלְּךָ" (Psalms 121:5 — "God is your shadow"), God's conduct with a person mirrors that person's own conduct. Therefore: if a person changes their ways, even an unfavorable sign changes. Hoshana Rabbah, as the day of final sealing, is precisely the moment when sincere teshuvah can transform any decree.

---

### 18. The Extended Window of Rachamim — From Elul to Hoshana Rabbah

**The Baruch Moshe (Rabbi Moshe of Satmar)** offers an elaborate parable of a great merchant king who periodically visits his trading partners and extends credit. At the annual accounting, many cannot repay their debts. The wise king forgives past debts and announces new terms. One merchant, however, approaches the king privately and says: "Don't just forgive me — let us examine the accounts carefully. You give me insufficient stock to turn a profit. From today forward, supply me abundantly, and I will repay everything, old and new."

The nimshal: Israel asks God not merely for forgiveness of past sins (though that too), but for a **רַבָּה** — an abundance of heavenly flow (הַשְׁפָּעָה) in children, life, and sustenance — so that we will have the capacity and the calm needed to fulfill His will properly. This is the meaning of "Hoshana **Rabbah**" — we request a large/great salvation specifically because we need the conditions to enable genuine service.

---

### 19. The Seven Hakafot and the Seventy Nations

**The Maor VaShemesh (Rabbi Kalonymus Kalman Epstein)** teaches:

The word **חוֹתָם** (seal) has the same letters as **חוֹמַת** (wall/fortress). Through the seven hakafot (circuits) performed on Hoshana Rabbah, Israel builds spiritual walls around the **מַלְכוּת** (Kingdom of God/Shechina), preventing the סִטְרָא אַחֲרָא from drawing nurture from it. The Shechina, thus protected, can ascend on Shemini Atzeret to unite with אֵין סוֹף בָּרוּךְ הוּא, and great חֲסָדִים flow to כְּנֶסֶת יִשְׂרָאֵל.

**The Bnei Yissaschar** connects the seven hakafot to the seventy bulls offered during all of Sukkot (Sukkah 55b — for the seventy nations). On each day of Sukkot one hakafah is performed to subdue ten of the seventy שָׂרִים (celestial princes of the nations). On Hoshana Rabbah, all seven hakafot are done together to subdue all seventy at once. He also connects this to the story of David and Goliath, suggesting that battle occurred on Hoshana Rabbah, and that David's staff was the lulav (which the Zohar calls "weapons of war") while the five smooth stones were the five aravot of Hoshana Rabbah — all preparation for the future war of Gog and Magog, which will also occur on Hoshana Rabbah.

---

### 20. The Hakafot as "Credit" (Hakafah)

**The Siach Yaakov Yosef (Rabbi Yaakov Yosef of Spinka)** makes a wordplay: **הַקָּפָה** (circuit/encircling) is also the Talmudic term for buying on credit (Avot 3:16 — "הַחֲנוּת פְּתוּחָה וְהַחֶנְוָנִי מַקִּיף" — the shop is open and the storekeeper extends credit). On Hoshana Rabbah — having accumulated the spiritual capital of Rosh Hashanah, the Ten Days of Repentance, Yom Kippur, and all of Sukkot — we request to "borrow on credit" the merits and energies of all these days to sustain us through the entire coming year.

The seven hakafot correspond to the seven shepherds (שִׁבְעָה רוֹעִים — Avraham, Yitzchak, Yaakov, Moshe, Aharon, Yosef, and David), and through the merit of their deeds we hope to be inscribed among the completely righteous.

---

### 21. Hoshana Rabbah as a Yom Tov on a Weekday — Sweetening of Judgments

**The Torat Emet (Rabbi Yehudah Leib Eiger of Lublin)** identifies three days that are treated as Yom Tov despite being weekdays: Erev Yom Kippur, Purim, and Hoshana Rabbah. All three share a common spiritual root: in each, something that began at the lowest level is transformed into good.

He then offers a profound comparison:

- **Yom Kippur**: The Torah explicitly promises atonement, but we fast on the day itself; the eve is the celebration.
- **Purim**: The Ari z"l says Yom Kippur is "כְּיוֹם פּוּרִים" (like Purim). On Purim, the salvation was visible in actuality (בְּחוּשׁ) — "the thing that was overturned for them" — whereas on Yom Kippur the atonement is believed but not tangibly felt. Each Jewish soul must reach the level of certainty that they are genuinely a new creation, as tangible as the Purim miracle.
- **Hoshana Rabbah**: This is the ultimate sweetening of all judgments (הַמְתָּקַת כָּל הַדִּינִים) — it is David HaMelech's aspect, the lowest level transformed into the highest good. Hence it is also called Yom HaAravah — the five willow-branches hint at the five Gevurot which are sweetened through the ritual beating. The Zohar on this night states: "Israel completes its judgments on this day."

---

### 22. The Night of Hoshana Rabbah — Tikkun and the Reading of Mishneh Torah

Multiple sources describe the widespread practice of staying awake all night learning, with special focus on the **תִּקוּן** (structured learning program for Hoshana Rabbah night) which includes Sefer Mishneh Torah and Sefer Tehillim:

**The Ben Ish Chai (Rabbi Yosef Chaim of Baghdad)** writes: at midnight on Hoshana Rabbah night, the outer seal is completed in the heavenly realm (the inner seal having been completed on Yom Kippur). Therefore one must arouse themselves to great teshuvah throughout this entire day. He describes in detail: learning the structured tikkun joyfully, reciting Shema before midnight, and saying Birchot HaShachar after midnight (but not Birkat HaTorah until after dawn).

**The Chida (Rabbi Chaim Yosef David Azulai)** confirms this widespread practice and adds the tradition of the Arizal's students to be very careful in one's deeds on this day. Learning Torah all night saves one from karet — and the morning prayer should be recited with full kavanah.

**Reb Noson of Breslov**: Tehillim is recited on this night because it is filled with cries and supplications to God. He says there is no person in the world who cannot find themselves in Tehillim — it encompasses every human being in every generation and every spiritual level, down to the very lowest. Tehillim is specifically efficacious for teshuvah. He connects this to Mishneh Torah — which he says circles around one core teaching: never despair of crying out to God, no matter what has happened, because God never abandons His people and His compassion never ends.

The reading of Mishneh Torah on Hoshana Rabbah night also alludes to the 490 (ת"צ) hours from Rosh Hashanah to Hoshana Rabbah morning — the gematria of תָּמִים ("perfect/complete") — suggesting that throughout all these hours one must be perfect (תָּמִים) with God.

---

### 23. "H' Tzilecha" — God's Conduct as Mirror

**The Lachazot Beno'am** closes the Hoshana Rabbah section by returning to the Baal Shem Tov's teaching on "ה' צִלְּךָ" (God is your shadow, Psalms 121:5): God acts toward a person as a mirror of how that person acts. Therefore:

- A merciful person will be treated mercifully from heaven.
- A person who changes their behavior changes their shadow — and thereby transforms any negative sign or decree.
- The Hoshana Rabbah shadow-sign in moonlight is not a fixed fate but a reflection of one's current spiritual state, changeable through genuine renewal.

---

### 24. Removal of the Ring from the Lulav — Multiple Meanings

The anthology dedicates substantial space to the custom of removing the binding ring from the lulav at the end of Hoshana Rabbah. Selected interpretations:

**The Amrei No'am (Rabbi Meir of Dzikhov):** The lulav is called the Kadosh Baruch Hu (Vayikra Rabbah 30:9 — "lulav zeh HaKadosh Baruch Hu"). The ring (טַבַּעַת) is the king's seal. Removing it from the lulav on Hoshana Rabbah represents the King passing His seal to the Tzaddikim and saying: "Write for the Jews as you see fit" — giving Israel the power to determine their own verdict. The ring also has the same gematria as "yamin smol" (right and left) — indicating both good and adverse verdicts are handed over. Additionally טַבַּעַת is from the root טֶבַע (nature) — removing the ring signals that from now on we request our life to be conducted **above the level of nature**, in open divine providence, especially looking toward the ultimate great salvation of Hoshana Rabbah which will be the day of Gog and Magog.

**The Yeitav Panim (Rabbi Yekutiel Yehudah of Sigot):** The custom represents the plight of Israel in exile — scattered and torn among the nations, forced to abandon Torah. At the moment of Hoshana Rabbah when the King is about to depart, we remove the lulav's binding and show it bare and dispersed — just as we are in exile, unable to fulfill God's will properly. We cry out: "See us! In the previous days when you visited we showed ourselves clothed in the mitzvot's splendor — but that was only for your eyes. In truth, all year we are oppressed and cannot serve You. Have mercy and do not leave us in their hands any longer."

---

### 25. The Aravah Beating — Its Deeper Meaning

**The Torat Emet:** The five willow branches beaten on Hoshana Rabbah correspond to the five Gevurot (severe judgments) which are sweetened through the beating — completing the process of המתקת הדינים that has been building throughout the entire holiday season.

**The Chatam Sofer's student**, citing the Chatam Sofer himself: the four species in the lulav correspond to four types of Jews (with and without Torah, with and without deeds). Throughout Sukkot we bound the aravah together with the others, including even those without Torah and deeds. But on Hoshana Rabbah we take the aravah alone — signifying those who have separated themselves entirely from the community. Their only remedy is to be struck — for Gog and Magog and Amalek, who will not return and will not join the unified bundle, there is no remedy but the blow.

**The Tiferet Shlomo (Rabbi Shlomo of Radomsk):** When the aravah is bound with the lulav bundle, it is elevated by the other species (the Tzaddik's power to elevate even those without merit). But on Hoshana Rabbah when it is taken alone — separated from the Tzaddik — it cannot receive a blessing. Hence "chavit chavit v'lo bereich" — it is beaten and no blessing is recited, because one who is separated from the Tzaddik cannot receive blessing.
